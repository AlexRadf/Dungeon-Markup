:::pitch
Eldoria surveyed the west once, in 1189. What came back was paper describing a country that is not there, and eleven men who were not asked about it again. The charter was never withdrawn -- only filed, under a department that no longer meets. It still pays by the hex, in coin, on production of a mark. You are the field parties. The map is blank because nobody has come back with it filled.
!! THEME -- the map is the campaign. There is no plot out there. There is distance, and what distance costs.
:::

:::rooms | # | THE SEVEN RULES OF THE LEDGER
1 | THE TABLE PICKS THE NIGHT | Players choose the date and post it, and whoever is free signs it. The GM never schedules, never chases, and never runs for nobody.
2 | THE PARTY IS WHOEVER CAME | Three to six of whoever signed. No standing party, no character who has to be there, and no story that falls over when someone stays home.
3 | ONE HEX A NIGHT | A session is one hex reached, opened and left. Charter it at the Ledger out loud, before anyone rolls anything, so the whole table knows the shape of the night.
4 | YOU END WHERE YOU BEGAN | Every session starts and finishes in Kettlewick. Not back by the second whistle is not a cliffhanger: it is a character still out there, and the next party may go and look.
5 | DISTANCE IS DIFFICULTY | Nothing out there scales to you. Ring one is a first level's night out. Ring four kills a ninth without discussion. The map is the difficulty setting and the party sets it themselves.
6 | WHAT ONE PARTY LEARNS, EVERY PARTY KNOWS | Filed hexes go on the table map in ink, where all the players can read them. Rumours, maps and warnings pass between characters who have never met.
7 | THE UNIVERSITY PAYS FOR PAPER | Not for kills. Coin, advancement and the next charter come out of hexes filed and marks set, and treasure counts for nothing until it is inside Kettlewick.
:::

The stones the tariff calls *marks* are not property boundaries and never were. Every one is set on a line, the line is unbroken from the Cold Teeth to the Burn, and the department that pays for them is the Office of Somnolent Maintenance -- decommissioned, unstaffed, and still, on paper, holding the west shut.

## How a night runs

:::beats
1. **Charter it.** At the Ledger, before anything else: name the hex out loud, by number. Slake writes it down. That number is tonight.
2. **Kit and party.** Whoever came. Rations by the day, a lamp, a mark from the quarry if they mean to be paid double, and one person willing to draw.
3. **Walk out.** Watches, not hours -- the travel table below. Every watch, a d6: on a 1, something finds them.
4. **Open the hex.** One watch to cross it, one more to find what is actually in it. The gazetteer entry is the site; everything before it is the walk.
5. **The whistle.** Kettlewick blows at dusk and again at full dark. Turning for home is a decision the players make, out loud, with the map in front of them.
6. **Walk back.** Same watches, same d6, and now they are carrying something.
7. **File it.** At the counter, in front of everyone. The hex goes on the table map in ink, the tariff is paid, and the night is over on a page rather than a cliff.
:::

:::cols 2
### The march

A day is **four watches**: three walked, one spent making and breaking camp. A hex is **six miles**, and what it costs to cross is what is in it.

| GROUND | WATCHES | LOST ON |
|---|---|---|
| Heath, sheep country | 1 | — |
| Hills, chalk, spoil | 2 | DC 12 |
| Forest, old wood | 2 | DC 14 |
| Marsh, fen, causeway | 3 | DC 14 |
| Mountain, scree, pass | 3 | DC 15 |
| Open water | boat, or go round | DC 13 |

**Lost** is one Survival check per hex entered off a road, made by whoever is drawing. Fail and the party arrives in a neighbouring hex, chosen by the GM, and the watch is spent. A road, a river bank, a guide or a hex already filed cancels the check outright -- which is the whole argument for filing them.

### The night's clocks

:::track
Daylight (watches): 4
Rations per head: 6
Lamp oil: 4
Lost: 3
:::

:::valve
If it's dragging: The weather turns. Cross now, in it, or lose the watch and the daylight with it.
If it's going fast: They reach the site with three watches in hand -- offer the neighbouring hex, unchartered, at their own risk and half tariff.
If they are out too late: Blow the second whistle in the distance, from the wrong direction, and let them work out what that means.
:::
:::

:::box gold | THE TABLE'S COPY OF THE MAP
Print sheet W2 twice. **The table's copy** stays face up in the middle of the table all campaign -- a hex is inked in when it is filed, circled when it is marked, initialled by the player who drew it. **Your copy** lives behind the screen with the gazetteer.

By the end of ring two the players have made something that looks like a real survey, because it is one, and they start arguing about which blank hex to open next with no prompting at all. That argument is the campaign.
:::

:::roll d8 | EVERY WATCH, ON A 1 IN 6 -- THEN ROLL HERE
1 Weather, and it means it. One watch lost, or push on at DC 13 CON.
2 Tracks, a day old, going the way you are going.
3 Another party's cold camp, marked with their charter number.
4 Something following at the edge of sight. It does not close while you look.
5 A person, alone, west of where any person should be alone.
6 Ring encounter -- roll on the table for the ring you are standing in.
7 A mark. Standing, fallen or broken. Note which; the Ledger pays for the answer.
8 Nothing, loudly. No birds for the rest of the watch.
:::

## The tariff

The university pays **per character, per hex filed**, on the night it is filed. A hex is *filed* when the party has crossed it, named what is in it at the counter, and drawn it. It is *marked* when they have also set a survey stone -- blanks are cut at the quarry in 0401 and weigh forty pounds each, which is the point.

| RING | HEXES | OUT AND BACK | BUILT FOR | FILED | MARKED |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| One | 10 | One day | Levels 1–2 | 50 gp · 200 XP | Double |
| Two | 10 | Two days | Levels 3–5 | 150 gp · 600 XP | Double |
| Three | 10 | Three to four days | Levels 6–8 | 400 gp · 1,800 XP | Double |
| Four | 4 | Five days, if you are quick | Levels 9–11 | 1,000 gp · 5,000 XP | Double |

:::cols 2
:::box gold | THREE THINGS THE TARIFF DOES
**Treasure counts in Kettlewick, not in the ground.** Coin, art and relics are worth their value in XP the moment they are inside the gate, and nothing at all while they are still out there in a sack.

**Nobody is paid for kills.** A hex crossed without a fight pays exactly what a hex crossed through a fight pays. Parties work this out in about two sessions and the campaign is better for it.

**A hex pays once.** The second party to file 0203 gets a look from Slake and no warrant. The map is finite, which is what makes it a race.
:::

### Between sessions

One downtime move per character, per week of game time, written in the Ledger and resolved in a sentence at the top of the next night.

- **Carouse.** Spend 25 gp per level. Roll d6: on a 5–6 a rumour, on a 1 a debt, otherwise a good week.
- **Study the filings.** Read every hex filed since you last did. Take advantage on the first Lost check of your next session.
- **Work the counter.** 20 gp a week, and you hear what everyone filed.
- **Mend.** Recover a level of exhaustion, a lost limb's worth of scar, or one point of the thing the fen did to you.
- **Recruit.** Hire a hireling from the ring you have already filed. Nobody will walk into a ring the town has never seen.
- **Ask.** One question to the Ledger, answered from what has been filed. Slake will not speculate, ever, about anything.
:::
