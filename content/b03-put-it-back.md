:::pitch
Beast Husbandry took an egg off a griffon's nest with all the forms filed, and has now decided the forms were a mistake. It goes back up four hundred feet of crag before the mother finishes counting, and it is nine days from hatching.
!! THEME -- a heist run backwards, against a clock nobody wrote down honestly.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
A specimen was collected from the Griffon's Roost by proper procedure with all forms filed. The acquisition is now judged unsound.

It goes back to the nest it came from, in the condition it left, before the female finishes her circuit and works out where it went. She is currently working through Stonehaven's flocks. She will not stop at sheep.

Speed matters more than stealth. Slightly.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
The Roost is a day and a half north, on the crags above the Stonehaven pass. One breeding pair, nesting eleven years. The female hunts a four-day circuit -- Stonehaven farmers say six, and they are the ones losing sheep.

Griffons take lambs, not people, until they do. A university van went up the north road at the end of last month and came back light. Beast Husbandry says otherwise. The climb is worse than the birds.
:::

*In pencil along the bottom edge, another hand:* Hairline on the shell after the second night. Reported. Told to keep it warm.

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- ANATOMICAL PLATE
From a Beast Husbandry textbook: *GRIFFON, EGG AND NEAR-TERM CHICK.* Two engraved figures, a ruled scale bar between them, callouts (a) to (f) keyed to a caption block. Fig. 1 is the egg in section -- shell, membrane, chick coiled inside, hatching-spur at the beak tip lettered (d). Fig. 2 is the chick at term, to the same scale bar, and that is where it lands: four feet, beak to tail. Caption for (d): *hatching-spur; retained in the adult; see p.211 on handling.* Someone has ringed it in pencil: *this is what made the crack.*
:::

:::secrets
1. The egg is nine days from hatching. The crack opens on the climb, not before, and the spur is what made it.
2. Her circuit is six days, not four. Husbandry wrote four on the notice to price the job cheap.
3. The male is feeding four eggs alone. He is starving, he will not leave the ledge, and he is why nothing has been eaten yet.
4. Ossoway took the egg on Draconics' written instruction. Draconics now denies instructing it and the instruction still exists.
5. A hatched chick imprints on whoever it sees first, permanently, and Husbandry knows this and has budgeted for it.
6. Nobody at Stonehaven has lost a lamb in nine days. That is not good news. That is her being somewhere else.
7. The old nests are occupied. Not by griffons.
8. The crate has been inverted at least once, and the pencil note is the only record of it.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT AT THE BOTTOM OF THE CLIMB
The crate is roped to a pony and the pony hates it, in the way animals hate a thing they can hear. Four hundred feet of crag above you, the nest two-thirds of the way up, and not one bird in the sky -- so she is out on her circuit, and you do not know how far along.
:::

:::beats
1. **The bottom of the crag.** No bird in the sky. Nobody can tell you how far into her circuit she is, and the notice says four days.
2. **The scree.** Loose, loud, the only daylight approach, and somebody has to carry the crate up it.
3. **The old nests.** Eleven years of bones sorted by season. Anyone who counts them learns the circuit is six days, not four.
4. **The crack opens.** Four rounds of blood heat and no inverting, on a rope, four hundred feet up.
5. **The male.** Smaller, starving, and he will not leave the ledge for anything. This is the fight if there is one.
6. **The nest**, four eggs and a gap, and the gap is the exact shape of what you are carrying.
7. **Down**, against the clock, with a chick or without one, and the pony is still where you left it or it is not.
:::

:::cols 2
:::place The Scree | loose, loud, and the only way up in daylight
- Every third step goes, and the sound of it carries the full four hundred feet.
- No anchors, no line, nothing to tie to for the first hundred and fifty.
- From here you can see the notch. From the notch, whatever is on the ledge can see you.
:::

:::place The Old Nests | three abandoned ledges, eleven years of bones
- Lining robbed out of them recently and in a hurry, and taken upward.
- A half-plucked goat, three days dead, dragged up by something that could not carry it far.
- Bones sorted by season, eleven layers of them. Count them and you have the circuit.
:::

:::place The Nest | four eggs, one gap, and the shape of the gap
- Fresh lining, stolen from below, packed the way a bird packs when it is short-handed.
- Nine days of down-drift lying undisturbed across the ledge.
- The gap is warm. Something has been sitting over the space where the egg was.
:::

:::place The Male's Ledge | he does not hunt a circuit, he is here
- Ribs showing under the feathers. He has not eaten in nine days.
- Three kills rotting on the ledges below, none of them touched.
- He goes to the gap every hour and settles over nothing at all.
:::

:::place The Pass Below | Stonehaven's flocks, and how far along she is
- Shepherds who will tell you six days, flatly, because they are the ones paying for it.
- Nothing taken in nine days, which is not good news, and which nobody up here has worked out.
- From the scree you can see the whole pass, which means the whole pass can see you.
:::
:::

4 Wind off the pass, straight down the face.
5 A Stonehaven shepherd, following the van's tracks.
6 The thing on the middle ledge wakes up.

:::valve
If it's dragging: the crack opens a quarter-inch and something inside moves against the shell.
If it's going fast: a shadow crosses the face, twice, and does not come back.
Cliffhanger: they get down and the crate is warm, and light, and open.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- The climb, with the crate. Four hundred feet, and the crate must not invert.
- The male, who is smaller than her and worse, because he cannot afford to lose.
- The hairline crack. If it opens on the rope, somebody is holding a hatching griffon.
- Her, back early, which is the same thing as her being back on time.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Put it back before the crack opens, which is the job as written and the hardest way to do it.
- Let it hatch and carry the chick up. It imprints. That is a problem you get to keep.
- Hand the male the egg and go. He will take it. He will not forget you.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **She finishes the circuit** and lands, and she is a bird that counts.
2. **She works down the pass**, taking the dogs first, then the farmers.
3. **She follows the van's smell** to Oakhaven, because it is the last thing she smelled on her own ledge.
4. **She finds the loading bay**, which is inside the town, with the doors open, at eleven in the morning.
:::

:::puzzle The Hairline Crack | on the rope · four rounds, then it is a chick
LOOKS: A hair of a line across the shell, and it was not there when you tied the crate on.
SOLVE: Blood heat and no inversion. Someone climbs one-handed, or the crate goes up on a second line, slowly, while somebody keeps it warm against them.
FAIL: It hatches on the face. Four feet of griffon, wet, loud, and imprinted on whoever is holding it.
:::

:::item Ossoway's Instruction | Paper, one sheet -- his coat, or the loading bay
A Draconics requisition, signed and dated, ordering the collection of one viable egg from the Roost. Draconics has denied issuing it twice in writing. Producing it is worth considerably more than sixty gold to Beast Husbandry, and considerably more than that to the Proctor.
:::
