:::pitch
The wine cellar has had eleven bays since the university opened. Tuesday's stocktake recorded nineteen and Friday's recorded twenty-six. The wine in the new bays is, by every account, superb, and the Bursar will not serve the Chancellor a bottle he cannot account for.
!! THEME -- something down there has learned our handwriting, and is helpfully filling in the parts we left blank.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT AT THE TOP OF THE STAIR, WITH SALLOW NOT COMING
Eleven bays of dusty racking, exactly as they have stood for ninety-nine years, and then a twelfth that is colder, cleaner, and whose mortar is still wet enough to take a thumbprint. Your lights do not reach the far end. Nothing in here echoes the way the size of the room says it ought to.
:::

One door, one key, and eleven bays that have been eleven bays since the university opened. **Measure the building from outside.** It has not grown an inch, and it is not going to.

:::rooms | AREA | WHAT IS THERE
1 | The old cellar | Bays 1 to 11, dusty racking, four hundred bottles, exactly as always. As far as the stock book has ever been concerned, this is the whole cellar.
2 | The new bays | Bay 12 onward. Colder, cleaner, mortar still soft. Perfect labels in Sallow's own hand. The wine is superb.
3 | The counting drift | Where your chalk marks get politely re-marked, in your own hand, in places you have not been. The count is different behind you, every single time.
4 | The working face | Bays being added right now, on a trowel's rhythm. **The second stores man is in bay 24.** He is well. He is cheerful. He does not want to leave.
5 | The back wall | If you reach it. Nobody has since Thursday. It is not a wall, it is the year the builder has got to.
:::

:::secrets
1. Something at the far end is building bays and stocking them as fast as it can lay brick.
2. It copies: the labels, the ledger hand, the chalk marks, and eventually the people doing the counting.
3. Vintage: the year one hundred and forty. It is building forward in time, not sideways in stone.
4. The second stores man is in bay 24, entirely well, and will explain pleasantly why he is staying.
5. The stock book updates its own totals overnight, in Sallow's hand, correctly, in advance.
6. The wine is genuinely superb. That is not a trick, and it is precisely why this is dangerous.
:::

:::beats
1. **Thirty-one bays.** Then forty. Then it reaches the footings under the dining hall, which is a building with people standing on it.
2. **The Centennial Dinner is served out of bays that are not ours.**
3. **Two hundred people drink a vintage from the year one hundred and forty**, and enjoy it enormously.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- The count is different behind you every time, and everybody's count differs from everybody else's.
- Your chalk marks get re-marked, in your own hand, in places you have not been yet.
- Wet mortar, cold air and very good wine, for three hours, in the dark.
- The stores man who followed you down and is being terribly cheerful about all of it.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Wall up the stair and lose the wine, which the Bursar will authorise and never forgive.
- Reach the working face and stop the builder, which is the only ending where everybody comes back up.
- Bring out a true count and let the Bursar decide what he is serving two hundred people.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat The Cellarer | CR 5 | AC 16 | HP 114 | SPD 30 ft, burrow 15 ft | STR +5 | DEX +1 | CON +5 | INT +2 | WIS +2 | CHA +3
**Two tools** +7, 5 ft: 12 (2d8+5) bludgeoning. A trowel and a mallet, used exactly as a builder uses them.
**Copies the hand.** Reproduces any writing, label or chalk mark it has seen, perfectly, inside a minute.
**Builds.** On its turn it may add five feet of finished, stocked, labelled bay in any direction, including behind you.
> The working face · one · it stops building the moment it is stopped, and not a second sooner
:::

:::stat The Cheerful Stores Man | CR 2 | AC 13 | HP 45 | SPD 30 ft | STR +3 | DEX +1 | CON +3 | INT +0 | WIS +0 | CHA +2
**He is well.** He will say so. He will keep saying so. He is warm, pleased to see you, and stacking.
**Hand** +5, 5 ft: 8 (1d10+3) bludgeoning, and only if somebody tries to walk him up the stair.
**Copied.** Answers every question about his own life correctly. He has been in bay 24 since Tuesday and has not eaten.
> Bay 24 · one · the other one did not come back, and this is what came back instead
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::npc Sallow, Bursar's stores | Ninety-nine years of the same handwriting · will not come down
WANT: The count to be eleven. He is not being obstructive, he is being certain.
VOICE: Precise, quiet, a clerk's voice. Answers in figures wherever a figure will do.
BREAK: Somebody has been signing his name in his book for three days and getting it right.
LINE: Eleven bays. It has been eleven bays my whole career and my father's whole career.
:::

:::puzzle The True Count | anywhere below · arithmetic, in the dark, against something that copies
LOOKS: Bays that are there counting in and gone counting back, and chalk that has been helpfully corrected.
SOLVE: Measure the building from outside. Ninety feet of dining hall cannot hold thirty-one bays, and that number does not change however the cellar counts.
FAIL: Never a dead end -- but a party that only counts inside brings up a number it cannot defend.
:::

:::item One Bottle, Your Pick | Wine, extraordinary -- any new bay, and part of the fee
Genuinely superb: the best thing anybody at this table will drink this year. Bottled at an estate that is unmistakably this university with one tower too many, in the year one hundred and forty, which is forty-one years from now. The Bursar's office will pay the seventy gold and let you keep it, and nobody in the Bursar's office will drink one.
