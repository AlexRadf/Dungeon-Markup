:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
The wine cellar has eleven bays. It has had eleven bays since the university opened. Tuesday's stocktake recorded nineteen. Friday's recorded twenty-six.

The wine in the new bays is very good. The wine in the new bays is not ours.

The Centennial Dinner is in six days and this office will not serve the Chancellor a bottle it can't account for.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
One door, one key, under the dining hall. Two stores staff went down on Tuesday and one came back -- he is well, he is cheerful, he is too cheerful.

The new bays are colder than the old and their mortar is still wet. Their labels are written in the same hand as the real stock. The stock book has begun updating its own totals overnight. Nobody has found the back wall since Thursday. Sallow has the only key and will not use it.
:::

:::box dark | HANDOUT -- A PAGE OF THE STOCK BOOK, DECADES OF ONE CLERICAL HAND
*Year 97* -- bays **11** -- bottles **412** -- *sig. Sallow* · *Year 98* -- bays **11** -- bottles **408** -- *sig. Sallow* · *Year 99* -- bays **11** -- bottles **400** -- *sig. Sallow*

*Tuesday* -- bays **19** -- bottles **1,144** -- *sig. Sallow* · *Friday* -- bays **26** -- bottles **1,690** -- *sig. Sallow*

And at the foot, in ink still faintly wet: *This morning* -- bays **31** -- bottles **2,004** -- *sig. Sallow*

**Sallow didn't write that last line. Sallow hasn't been down since Tuesday. The handwriting is identical.**
:::
:::

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- LABEL
A wine label, actual size. Oval, engraved border, confident line work -- vine scroll, a small woodcut of a house on a hill, entirely convincing. The estate name is in letters that are *almost* the Eldorian alphabet; four of them are simply wrong and the eye keeps sliding off. Beneath, in perfectly ordinary lettering: **BOTTLED AT THE ESTATE · VINTAGE: THE YEAR ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY.** The university is in its ninety-ninth year. Draw the little house with real care and make it, on close inspection, this university -- same towers, same wall, one tower too many.
:::

:::secrets
1. Something at the far end is building bays and stocking them as fast as it can work.
2. It copies. The labels, the ledger hand, the chalk marks, and eventually the people doing the counting.
3. Year one hundred and forty. It is building forward in time, not sideways in stone.
4. The second stores man is in bay 24. He is well. He does not want to leave and he will say so pleasantly.
5. Measure the building from outside. It has not grown by an inch, and it is not going to.
6. The stock book updates its own totals overnight, in Sallow's hand, correctly, in advance.
7. The wine is genuinely very good. That is not a trick and it is the reason this is dangerous.
8. Sallow will not come down. He has the only key and he has had it in his pocket since Tuesday.
:::

One door, one key, and eleven bays that have been eleven bays since the university opened. **Light:** what you carry, and it does not reach the far end of a room ninety feet long. **Measure the building from outside.** It has not grown an inch.

:::rooms | AREA | WHAT IS THERE
1 | The old cellar | Bays 1 to 11, dusty racking, four hundred bottles, exactly as always. This is the whole cellar as far as the stock book has ever been concerned.
2 | The new bays | Bay 12 onward. Colder, cleaner, mortar still wet enough to take a thumbprint. Perfect labels in Sallow's hand. The wine is very good.
3 | The counting drift | Where your chalk marks get re-marked, in your own hand, in places you have not been. The count is different behind you, every time.
4 | The working face | Bays still being added, right now, on a trowel's rhythm. **The second stores man is in bay 24.** He is well. He is cheerful. He does not want to leave.
5 | The back wall | If you get to it. Nobody has since Thursday. It is the year the builder has reached, not a wall.
:::

:::roll d6 | COMPLICATION -- ONCE PER BAY THEY COUNT
1 The count is one higher than last time.
2 A chalk mark you did not make, in your hand.
3 A cork pops somewhere behind you.
4 The stores man offers you a bottle.
5 The lights do not reach as far as they did.
6 A finished label, still wet, on new mortar.
:::

:::track
Days to the Centennial Dinner: 6
Bays counted and rising: 12
:::

:::valve
If it's dragging: the lights go out at the far end and come back on nearer.
If it's going fast: a bay is added behind them, between them and the stair, while they are talking.
Cliffhanger: the stock book upstairs has tomorrow's total in it, and one of their names in the margin.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- The count is different behind you, every time, and everybody's count is different from everybody else's.
- Your chalk marks get re-marked, in your own hand, in places you have not been yet.
- The fumes. Wet mortar, cold air and very good wine, for three hours, in the dark.
- The stores man who followed you down and is very cheerful about all of it.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Wall the stair and lose the wine, which the Bursar will authorise and never forgive.
- Reach the working face and stop the builder, which is the only ending where anyone comes back up.
- Bring out a true count and let the Bursar decide what he is serving two hundred people.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat The Cellarer | CR 5 | AC 16 | HP 114 | SPD 30 ft, burrow 15 ft | STR +5 | DEX +1 | CON +5 | INT +2 | WIS +2 | CHA +3
**Two tools** +7, 5 ft: 12 (2d8+5) bludgeoning. A trowel and a mallet, used exactly as a builder uses them.
**Copies the hand.** It reproduces any writing, label or chalk mark it has seen, perfectly, within a minute.
**Builds.** On its turn it may add five feet of finished, stocked, labelled bay in any direction, including behind you.
**Cannot be outrun in its own cellar.** Every corridor here is one it made.
> The working face · one · it stops building the moment it is stopped, and not one second before
:::

:::stat The Cheerful Stores Man | CR 2 | AC 13 | HP 45 | SPD 30 ft | STR +3 | DEX +1 | CON +3 | INT +0 | WIS +0 | CHA +2
**He is well.** He will say so. He will keep saying so. He is warm, he is pleased to see you, and he is stacking.
**Hand** +5, 5 ft: 8 (1d10+3) bludgeoning, only if somebody tries to take him up the stair.
**Copied.** He answers every question about his life correctly. He has been in bay 24 since Tuesday and has not eaten.
> Bay 24 · one · the other one did not come back and this is what came back instead
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::npc Sallow, Bursar's stores | Ninety-nine years of the same handwriting · will not come down
WANT: The count to be eleven. He is not being obstructive. He is being certain.
VOICE: Precise, quiet, a clerk's voice. Answers in figures.
BREAK: Somebody has been signing his name in his book for three days and getting it right.
LINE: Eleven bays. It has been eleven bays my whole career and my father's whole career.
USE: He has the key, the book, and ninety-nine years of true totals to measure against.
:::

:::puzzle The True Count | anywhere below · arithmetic, in the dark, against something that copies
LOOKS: Bays that are there when you count in and not when you count back, and chalk that has been helpfully corrected.
SOLVE: Measure the building from outside. Ninety feet of dining hall cannot hold thirty-one bays, and that number does not change however the cellar counts.
FAIL: Never a dead end -- but a party that only counts inside will bring up a number it cannot defend to the Bursar.
:::
:::

:::item One Bottle, Your Pick | Wine, extraordinary -- any new bay, and it is part of the fee
Genuinely very good: the best thing anybody at this table will drink this year. Bottled at an estate that is this university with one tower too many, in the year one hundred and forty, which is forty-one years from now. The Bursar's office will pay the seventy gold and let you keep it. Nobody in the Bursar's office will drink one.
:::
