:::pitch
Something has walked forty feet behind a night technician every night for three weeks. It has never come closer and it has never fallen back. She would like to be walked home. That is the whole job, it pays twelve gold, and it is worth doing properly.
!! THEME -- it is not following her. It is walking her off site, and nobody has told it it can stop.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
I finish at two in Chemistry and I walk home across the park. For three weeks something has walked behind me. It does not come closer. It does not go away. It stops when I stop.

Security walk me to the east gate. Past the gate is not their ground. It is eleven more minutes to my door and I would like company for them.

I can pay twelve gold and I will cook you something. -- **D. Ashgrove**, technician, Chemistry, nights
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
The physics path lamps have been out since Michaelmas and Estates have the requisition pending a section nobody will fill in.

Forty feet, every night, never closer. It stops at the east gate and stands there until she is out of sight. The night porter's post was cut in a budget round six years ago and the lodge has been dark since. Two other people finish after midnight on that side and neither has ever been followed. A gardener says he has seen a lamp go along the park at two and nobody carrying it.
:::

:::secrets
1. It has never been closer than forty feet and never further. Three weeks, every night, in all weathers.
2. It is not following her. It is walking the last person off site, which is the job it was made for.
3. It cannot pass the east gate. It stops there and waits until she is out of sight, and then it goes back and starts again.
4. Look straight at it and it sits down. It will not move again until nobody is looking.
5. The porter's post was cut six years ago. The hound was never stood down, because standing it down is a form and the form went with the post.
6. Deema is not the first. It walked a lecturer home for two years and she never told anybody, because she rather liked it.
7. Whoever is last off site is who it walks. If the party stays later than her, it walks them instead, and it will do it forever.
8. Ivet Sunn, who kept the lodge, is alive in Oakhaven, drinks in the Coach and Horses, and would come up tomorrow if anybody thought to ask her.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- It will not be fought and cannot be caught. Every plan that starts with cornering it ends with it sitting down.
- If the party leaves after Deema, it transfers, and it does not transfer back.
- Security's remit ends at the gate and the officer on nights knows exactly what he did not write down.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Walk her home. Tonight is solved by walking eleven minutes. Do not let anyone talk the table out of this being enough.
- Stand it down: Ivet Sunn, in person, at the lodge door, in the words the post used. She will come up for the price of a drink.
- Relight the lodge. It stops walking anybody the moment somebody is on duty again, which costs the university nothing and will take Estates nine months.
:::
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT AT TWO, OUTSIDE CHEMISTRY, IN THE RAIN
She locks up, checks the door twice, and sets off without waiting to see if you follow. Forty feet back along the path, at the edge of what you can see, a lamp comes on. Nobody is carrying it. When she stops to shake out her umbrella, it stops.
:::

:::beats
1. **The park.** Eleven minutes of walking and a conversation she has been saving for three weeks. It keeps its forty feet. Say the distance out loud every time somebody checks.
2. **Somebody looks straight at it.** It sits down in the wet grass and does not move. Nothing you do makes it move. Turn your back and it is forty feet away again.
3. **The physics path, unlit.** Two stray dogs off the allotments, all noise and no plan. The lamp comes up to twenty feet for exactly as long as this takes, and then goes back to forty.
4. **The east gate.** It stops. It will not step through, and it will not be coaxed, called, bribed or carried through. It waits until she is out of sight down the lane.
5. **Past the gate is where the job is.** Eleven more minutes, her street, her door, her kitchen, and a hot meal at half past two in the morning.
6. **Tomorrow night is the actual question.** Someone has to be last off site, or somebody has to go and find Ivet Sunn.
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat The Lamp-Hound | CR 1/4 | AC 13 | HP 22 | SPD 40 ft | STR +1 | DEX +3 | CON +1 | INT -3 | WIS +2 | CHA +0
**It has never hurt anybody.** In six years, in every log, not once. It has no attack on this card because it has never needed one.
**Escort.** Keeps forty feet behind whoever left last. Closes to twenty while something threatens them, and only then.
**Seen.** Looked at directly, it sits and is immobile until unobserved. It is not hiding. It is embarrassed.
**Lamplight.** Sheds dim light in a 20 ft radius, which on the physics path is the only light there is.
> The park, nightly, at two · never rolls initiative unless the table insists, and then it loses on purpose
:::

:::npc Deema Ashgrove | Human · night technician, Chemistry · six years of the two o'clock walk
WANT: To get home without her heart going. That is all. She has not asked for anything else.
VOICE: Tired, dry, funny at her own expense. Talks the whole way because the silence is worse.
BREAK: Somebody offering to do it again tomorrow. She was not expecting a second night.
LINE: I'm not frightened of it. I'm frightened of it being there again, which is different.
USE: She will feed you, and she knows every night-shift name on that side of campus.
:::

:::roll d6 | THE ELEVEN MINUTES
1 It rains harder. The lamp does not go out.
2 A fox crosses. It ignores the fox entirely.
3 Her umbrella turns inside out. It stops while she fights it.
4 The gate is locked -- DC 12 to lift the latch, or the wall.
5 A first-year, lost, drunk, wants directions to a hall that burned down.
6 It is thirty-five feet, not forty. Only once. Nobody can be sure.
:::

If it's going fast: she asks, on her doorstep, what happens tomorrow. Let them answer it in character.
Cliffhanger: they walk back through the park at three and the lamp is behind *them*, at forty feet, all the way to their door.
