:::pitch
Three survey teams went to the Iron Henge and one tripod came back, stripped. Metallurgy wants its theodolite. Metallurgy does not want to send anybody it would miss.
!! THEME -- the department has already decided what you are worth, and printed it on the notice.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
Three teams sent to the Iron Henge. One tripod came back with the brass stripped off it. The instruments were department property. The staff were on fixed-term contracts.

Find what is taking our equipment. Stop it taking any more. We are not asking anyone to be brave. We are asking for someone replaceable in a cheaper bracket.

Bring back the theodolite if there is anything left of it.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
The pillars are not iron. Everyone calls them iron. Eleven of them, black, taller than a person, in the hills two days north -- though farmers who use the pass swear there are nine.

Compasses spin within fifty yards. Sheep won't graze there. Metallurgy has lost three teams in a year, or one team three times, depending who's telling it. Cartography says the henge moves. Cartography says that about everything.
:::

Draw them smooth and faintly concentric, like river stones -- stipple the surface, hatch the shadows. They are lumps of digested metal and the eye should take a second to get there. :::secrets
1. The pillars are not iron and never were. They are shed casings, eleven moults deep, standing where they were left.
2. It is one creature, not a swarm. Old, cart-sized, and slow for an hour after it feeds.
3. Perch is alive in the trench, pinned under the breastplate that saved him from being eaten with it.
4. It has never touched anyone in wool. Every team that died went out hung with brass instruments.
5. Cartography is right: the ring moves one pillar a year. The farmers who say nine are counting the ones still standing.
6. Metallurgy lost three teams. Finance is holding the claim because Section 4 is blank, and Section 4 is blank because nobody will write the word.
7. Compasses spin off the casings, not off ore. The pillars are magnetised by what grew them.
8. Nothing grows inside the ring. That is why the sheep won't cross it, and it has nothing to do with fear.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT, THEN LET THEM WALK
Eleven black pillars a stone's throw apart, and the ground between them scattered with rings, buckles and nails, the way a beach is scattered with shell. Your compass is turning and will not stop turning. Anyone in mail has the hair standing up on their arms.
:::

:::web THE WEB -- FIVE PLACES, NO ORDER
Three clues into everywhere they have to reach. The ring is where they start; nothing has to lead there.
:::

:::cols 2
:::node A | The Ring | place | start
Open ground, no cover, metal litter to the ankle. Whatever happened here happened in the open and nobody ran far.
-> B  Guy-line stains west of the stones, tent-shaped, three seasons deep.
-> C  One furrow ploughed between two pillars, not dug, and the soil turned out of it is still warm.
-> D  The litter thins going north, and something has been going north with it.
:::

:::node B | The Camp | place
Three seasons of it, tents rotted to lace, and every single peg gone.
-> C  Only one bedroll unrolled. Somebody was still here after the others left, and his tracks go to the furrow.
-> D  Pegs, pots, buckles, rivets -- gone. The wool untouched. Follow what it wanted, not what it left.
-> E  Field notes: eleven pillars in year one. Ten in year two. Somebody was counting, and then stopped counting.
:::

:::node C | The Trench | place
A furrow between two pillars, still warm, and something at the bottom of it that is breathing.
-> B  Perch, pinned under his own breastplate, alive: *the camp -- my notes -- and take that mail off.*
-> D  The furrow runs on north and stays warm the whole way.
-> E  Perch knows what the pillars are and will say it exactly once. They are shed.
:::

:::node D | The Midden | place
Pellets of digested metal in a heap, some of them shield-sized, all of them faintly warm.
-> B  A theodolite, half dissolved, department number still legible -- and the loss form was filed from the camp.
-> C  One pellet holds a breastplate strap, cut through, not eaten. Something is still wearing the rest.
-> E  Measure a pellet against a pillar's bore. The animal that made these made the henge.
:::

:::node E | The Old One | creature
Cart-sized, eleven moults old, and under your feet the whole time. It does not hunt you. It hunts what you are carrying.
:::
:::

:::valve
If it's dragging: something takes a metal object off somebody, right now, and goes back under.
If it's going fast: the second pellet heap, further north, and it is fresher than this one.
Cliffhanger: they come back to Oakhaven and the loading-bay doors have been chewed.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- It eats worked metal, and a suit of armour is a plated lunch that cannot run.
- It burrows. No cover, no warning, and the ground is the ambush.
- Strike a pillar with steel and it rings like a bell. It comes to the bell.
- Perch is still out here and everything you do loudly happens near him.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Feed it a van-load of scrap and leave while it is busy being full.
- Kill it while it is gorged and slow, which is the only hour it can be killed at this level.
- Dig Perch out, because he is the only person alive who knows what the pillars are.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **It takes your metal at night**, a piece at a time, quietly, starting with whatever is furthest from the fire.
2. **Then the horses' tack.** Then the horses.
3. **It follows the road** to Oakhaven, because the road is where the metal goes.
4. **It finds the assay lab**, which is four hundred pounds of refined ore behind one door.
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat The Old One | CR 2 | AC 16 | HP 52 | SPD 30 ft, burrow 20 ft | STR +3 | DEX +0 | CON +3 | INT -4 | WIS +1 | CHA -3
**Bite** +5, 5 ft: 9 (2d6+2) piercing, and any metal armour the target wears takes a permanent −1 to AC.
**Ferrous appetite.** As a bonus action it takes one unattended metal object within 5 ft, or one held object on a failed DC 13 STR save. Eaten is eaten.
**Burrow ambush.** Surfacing costs it no movement. It has advantage against anyone who has not seen it this round.
SENSES: tremorsense 60 ft, and it smells worked metal at 120 ft
**Gorged.** After 50 lb of metal its speed halves and it loses the bite. That hour is the whole fight.
> Anywhere · one creature, never two · wool is invisible to it
:::

:::npc Alder Perch | Surveyor, third team · alive, pinned, nine days without water
WANT: Out from under his own breastplate, and then never to be issued equipment again.
VOICE: Cracked, slow, funny about it in a way that is worse than crying.
BREAK: He watched it take the other two and it did not take him. He knows exactly why and hates the answer.
LINE: It never wanted us. It wanted what we were carrying. We were carrying everything.
USE: The only source for the pillars, the wool, and the count. Free him and the job solves itself.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle The Ringing Pillar | anywhere in the ring · a bell, and a bell can be aimed
LOOKS: Strike a casing with steel and the whole ring hums for a count of nine.
SOLVE: It is bait. Ring a pillar at the far side, with the scrap piled under it, and buy yourselves the trench.
FAIL: Ring it while standing next to it and the ground opens under the person holding the steel.
:::

:::item The Theodolite of Field Party Three | Wondrous, uncommon -- the midden
Half dissolved, brass gone soft as candle-wax, still true. Sighting through it names the direction and distance of the nearest worked metal over five pounds. It is also the object the fee is for, and Finance will notice the difference.
:::
