:::pitch
Three survey teams went up to the Iron Henge. One tripod came back with the brass licked off it. Metallurgy has written off the teams, the theodolite and, quietly, the whole hillside, and is offering forty gold to anybody who fancies a look.
!! THEME -- it does not eat people. It eats cutlery. Everybody is fine and nobody has got round to telling Finance.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT, THEN LET THEM WANDER
Eleven black pillars a stone's throw apart, and the ground between them scattered with buckles, nails and eyelets the way a beach is scattered with shell. Your compass is spinning like a top. Anyone in mail has the hair standing up on both arms. Somewhere under all this, something enormous rolls over in its sleep.
:::

:::beats
1. **The ring.** Compasses spin, metal litter to the ankle, and sooner or later somebody notices that every single piece of it is a *fitting* -- buckles, nails, eyelets -- and that there is no cloth. Anywhere. And no bone at all.
2. **The camp.** Three seasons of tents rotted to lace, every peg gone, wool blankets folded and bone dry. The field notes count eleven pillars in year one and ten in year two, and then give up on counting.
3. **The trench.** Still warm, and Surveyor Perch alive at the bottom of it, pinned under his own breastplate, nine days without water and absolutely delighted to see anybody. He will tell them the two things that matter: the pillars are *shed*, and take the mail off.
4. **It takes something.** Not a person -- a kettle, at night, from the edge of the firelight, and nobody hears it go.
5. **The midden**, and the arithmetic: hold a pellet up against a pillar's bore. Same animal. Eleven times over, one a year, for as long as anybody's grandmother can remember.
6. **They choose.** Feed it a cartload of scrap and stroll off, or hit it while it is gorged and slow, or just dig Perch out and go home with the only man who understands the place.
:::

:::cols 2
:::place The Ring | open ground, no cover, and the compass will not settle
- Buckles, nails and eyelets to the ankle. No cloth, no bone, not so much as a tooth.
- Strike a pillar with steel and the whole ring hums for a count of nine.
- Warm on the north face and cold on the south, which is backwards, and which nobody can explain.
:::

:::place The Camp | three seasons of it, and every tent peg gone
- Guy-lines still knotted neatly round nothing whatsoever.
- One bedroll unrolled. Somebody stayed on a while after the others walked home.
- Wool blankets untouched and folded. The pots are gone to the last rivet.
:::

:::place The Trench | ploughed from below, between two pillars, and still warm
- Not dug. Ploughed, from underneath, and the turned soil steams in the cold.
- Perch, pinned, lucid, parched, and very funny about all of it.
- His breastplate is bent inward around him, which is exactly why he is still here.
:::

:::place The Midden | pellets of digested metal, some of them shield-sized
- Smooth and faintly concentric, like river stones, and warm to the hand.
- A theodolite half dissolved, department number still perfectly legible.
- One pellet has a signet ring fused into it. The owner would quite like it back.
:::
:::

:::secrets
1. The pillars were never iron. They are shed casings, eleven moults deep, standing where they were left.
2. It is one animal, not a swarm. Old, cart-sized, and slow as a barge for an hour after it feeds.
3. Nobody has been eaten. All three teams walked home over the hills in their socks, because it ate the nails out of their boots.
4. It has never touched anyone in wool. Every team it emptied went out hung with brass instruments.
5. Cartography is right and everyone laughs at them anyway: the ring moves one pillar a year.
6. Metallurgy's claim is held because Section 4 says *Casualties* and Metallurgy cannot bring itself to write *none, they walked*.
:::

+++

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- It eats worked metal, and a suit of plate is a packed lunch that cannot run away.
- It burrows. There is no cover out here and the ground itself is the ambush.
- Hit a pillar with steel and it rings like a bell, and it comes to the bell every time.
- Perch is still down that trench, and everything loud they do happens about nine feet above his head.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Feed it. A cartload of scrap buys an hour, and an hour is enough to do anything out here.
- Fight it gorged, which is the only hour at this level where that is a plan rather than a story.
- Dig Perch out and go. He is worth more than the theodolite and he knows it.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **It takes your metal at night**, one piece at a time, starting with whatever is furthest from the fire.
2. **Then the horses' tack.** The horses are fine. The horses are extremely relaxed about it.
3. **It follows the road** toward Oakhaven, because the road is where the metal goes.
4. **It finds the assay lab**, which is four hundred pounds of refined ore behind one wooden door.
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat The Old One | CR 2 | AC 16 | HP 52 | SPD 30 ft, burrow 20 ft | STR +3 | DEX +0 | CON +3 | INT -4 | WIS +1 | CHA -3
**Bite** +5, 5 ft: 9 (2d6+2) piercing, and any metal armour worn takes a permanent −1 AC. It is aiming for the armour. It keeps hitting the person by accident.
**Ferrous appetite.** Bonus action: takes one unattended metal object within 5 ft, or a held one on a failed DC 13 STR save. Eaten is eaten.
**Burrow ambush.** Surfacing costs no movement, with advantage on anyone who has not seen it this round.
SENSES: tremorsense 60 ft, and it smells worked metal at 120 ft
**Gorged.** After 50 lb of metal its speed halves and it loses the bite for an hour.
> Anywhere · one creature, never two · a party in wool is invisible to it
:::

:::npc Alder Perch | Surveyor, third team · pinned, parched, thrilled
WANT: Out from under his own breastplate, and never to be issued equipment again as long as he lives.
VOICE: Cracked, slow, and funnier than anybody nine days in a hole has any right to be.
BREAK: He has had nine days to work out why it left him alone and the answer is *wool*, and he finds this unbearable.
LINE: It never wanted us. It wanted what we were carrying. We were carrying everything.
USE: The pillars, the wool and the count all come from him. Free him and the job mostly solves itself.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle The Ringing Pillar | anywhere in the ring · a bell, and a bell can be aimed
LOOKS: Strike a casing with steel and the whole ring hums for a count of nine.
SOLVE: It is bait, and it is aimable. Ring a pillar on the far side with the scrap piled under it and buy yourselves the trench.
FAIL: Ring one while standing next to it and the ground opens under whoever is holding the steel.
:::

:::roll d6 | COMPLICATION
1 The compass needle snaps clean off.
2 A pellet is still hot enough to blister.
3 A pillar settles an inch. The ground remembers.
4 Sheep in the pass, and a farmer behind them.
5 Perch calls out, and it hears him.
6 Somebody's belt buckle goes missing.
:::

:::item The Theodolite of Field Party Three | Wondrous, uncommon -- the midden
Half dissolved, the brass gone soft as candle wax, and still dead true. Sight through it and it names the direction and distance of the nearest worked metal over five pounds. It is also the object the forty gold is for, and Finance will absolutely notice the difference.
:::

:::valve
If it's dragging: something takes a metal object off somebody's belt, right now, and goes straight back under.
If it's going fast: a second pellet heap further north, fresher than this one, and bigger.
Cliffhanger: they get back to Oakhaven and the loading-bay doors have been chewed.
:::
