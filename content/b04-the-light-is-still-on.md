:::pitch
The keeper of Lighthouse Rock has filed nothing for eleven weeks and the light has burned every night of them, exactly on pattern. Navigation needs to know who has been lighting it, because if the answer is nobody, the wrecks are the university's.
!! THEME -- something out there has learned what we do, and is doing it slightly better every night.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
Lighthouse Rock has filed no keeper's log in eleven weeks. The light runs nightly, on pattern, which is our difficulty.

If the light was showing, the wrecks are the pilots' liability. If it wasn't, they're ours, and we cannot afford ourselves.

Establish what has been lit and by whom. Bring back the log. The finding matters more than the rescue. We'd rather it didn't.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
Two days west to Highport, then an hour of open water and a rock that goes under at high tide. Maerin Vosk has kept the light nine years and has filed nothing for eleven weeks. It burns anyway, every night, exactly on pattern.

Two ships have gone down on the reef since -- Highport says three. Pilots steer by it regardless. Hydrological Sorcery ran tide work off that coast in March and denies all of it.
:::

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- NOTATION PAGE
A plate from the Highport pilots' light-list: the reference sheet showing every light's flash pattern so a helmsman can name it by counting. A column of notation strips -- long horizontal rules, solid black blocks for flashes, gaps for dark, ruled off at the second, labelled at the left. Fine engraved type, printed key at the foot. LIGHTHOUSE ROCK prints as three flashes, long dark, repeat. Below it, three strips ruled by hand in a pilot's pen: *as obs. 4th · as obs. 11th · as obs. 19th.* They match neither the print nor each other, and the blocks crowd toward the right end of each one. Beneath: *Three of us counted. We agreed. Then we counted again.*
:::

:::secrets
1. Vosk is alive on the gallery. He has not eaten in eleven weeks and he is still keeping the light.
2. The thing lights the lamp. It learned by watching him do it, four hundred times, through the glass.
3. It has started improvising. The new patterns are what the ships are wrecking on.
4. The oil has not gone down because it is not burning oil. Nothing up there is burning anything.
5. Hydrological Sorcery's tide work in March brought it up the reef. There is a receipt, and it is in Oakhaven.
6. The log's last entry is dated three weeks from today, in Vosk's hand, and Vosk has not written it yet.
7. It copies patterns, not meanings. It does not know what a light is for. It is about to work it out.
8. The door at the base is bolted from the inside because Vosk bolted it, on the seventh of March, and went up.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- The tide. Six hours, thirty yards of rock, and no high ground but the tower.
- It copies patterns, and that includes yours -- marching order, watch rotations, the way you knock.
- The light goes on at dusk whether or not you are ready to be on the gallery.
- Vosk, who is still keeping the light, and will fight to keep keeping it.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Kill the light and take the log. The finding is what the department asked for.
- Get Vosk down. He weighs nothing now and he does not want to come.
- Give it a pattern of your own, and watch very carefully what it does with it.
:::

:::beats
1. **It runs a new pattern tonight**, and it is a better pattern than last night's.
2. **A grain barge out of Highport** takes the new pattern for the old one and takes the reef with it.
3. **Pilots stop trusting every light on the coast**, which closes Highport more thoroughly than a war would.
4. **It learns a pattern that means come in**, and starts running that one instead.
:::

:::npc Maerin Vosk | Keeper, nine years · alive, eleven weeks unfed, entirely lucid
WANT: The light lit correctly. That is the whole of it and it has been for eleven weeks.
VOICE: Dry, courteous, hoarse. Answers every question and volunteers nothing.
BREAK: He knows what has been lighting it. He let it. It was that or the light went out.
LINE: I did not teach it. I only did the job in front of it, every night, until it could.
USE: He knows the true pattern, and he is the only witness the department's finding will accept.
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle The Pattern | the gallery · three flashes, long dark, repeat
LOOKS: The clockwork turns, the lens sweeps, and the count is wrong in a way that takes a full minute to be sure of.
SOLVE: Run the true pattern in front of it -- by hand, on the shutters -- until it copies the true one instead of its own. Anyone who has read the pilots' plate knows the count.
FAIL: It copies the last thing it saw. Show it a lantern waving and it will show a lantern waving, all night, to everything on the water.
:::
