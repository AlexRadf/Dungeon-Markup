:::pitch
Something on Grazier's Hill has howled from moonrise to dawn for eleven nights and three Rhetoric seniors have finals in nine days. They have pooled everything they own. It is eighteen gold, a coat and half a cheese, and it is genuinely everything they own.
!! THEME -- it is not howling. It is asking. Nobody has answered it in eleven nights.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
Something on Grazier's Hill has howled for eleven nights and we have finals in nine.

It starts at moonrise. It doesn't stop until dawn. We've tried earplugs. We've tried drinking. We've tried moving to the library, and the library closes.

We've pooled everything we have. Make it stop. We are past caring how.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
Eleven nights, always at moonrise, always ending at dawn. Grazier's Hill is university land leased to a sheep farmer.

Nothing has been taken -- not one animal, which the farmer finds worse than losses. A night security officer has been seen carrying a covered dish up the hill and coming back without it. Divinity keeps a silver handbell nobody is allowed to ring. Drunk students swear the howling has words in it.
:::

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- NOTATION PAGE
A ruled page from a music student's notebook. Five staves of hand-drawn notation, the ink getting scratchier down the page as the writer works faster. Above the notes, a phonetic transcription in a shaky hand. Then, in the bottom margin, large and pressed hard: *It is not howling. It is nine syllables. It is the same nine syllables. It has been the same nine syllables for eleven nights and I have been writing them down as MUSIC because I could not admit to myself that they were WORDS. I don't know the language. Divinity would know the language. I am not going to Divinity.* Draw the notation properly -- real staves, real note-heads, a time signature that changes three times. Leave the syllables just legible.

:::secrets
1. It is not howling. It is nine syllables, the same nine, and it is asking for something.
2. It cannot leave the hill and it cannot stop until it is answered.
3. Divinity's handbell answers it. Divinity will deny owning a bell, in writing, twice.
4. Security has left food on that stone for eleven nights on somebody's instruction and will not say whose.
5. Killing it works. It is back in four nights and starts again from the first syllable.
6. Not one sheep has been taken. The farmer has worked this hill thirty years and finds that worse than losses.
7. The music student wrote the syllables down and then stopped going up the hill. She is in Rhetoric's year.
8. Whatever it is asking for, the answer is nine syllables long as well.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT AT MOONRISE, AND DO NOT HURRY IT
It starts forty yards up the slope, from something you cannot see, and it does not move while it does it. Lower down there is a covered dish on a flat stone with the cloth still over it. The dish is warm. There are ring marks in the grass around the stone, eleven of them, one inside the next.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- It stops the moment you speak and starts the moment you stop. It will outlast the conversation.
- The farmer is out here too, with a dog and a lamp and thirty years of being right about this hill.
- It will not come to you. Whatever happens tonight happens in the hollow, on its ground.
- Dawn. It stops at dawn on its own, and then you have twenty-three hours and the same problem.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Get the bell out of Divinity, which is a theft, a favour or a very good argument.
- Say the nine syllables back, and mean it, which is harder than it sounds and is the real ending.
- Kill it, which works, and be somewhere else in four nights when it starts again from the first syllable.
:::

4. **Something else on the hill answers**, and it is not on the hill, and it is not nine syllables.

:::cols 2
:::stat The Asker | CR 2 | AC 13 | HP 45 | SPD 30 ft | STR +2 | DEX +3 | CON +2 | INT +1 | WIS +3 | CHA +4
**It does not attack first.** In eleven nights it has hurt nothing, taken nothing, and moved nowhere.
**Claw** +5, 5 ft: 7 (1d8+3) slashing, twice, and only if cornered in the hollow.
**The nine syllables.** Any creature that hears them and does not answer: DC 13 WIS or no benefit from that night's rest. That is the whole of the crime.
**Bound.** It cannot leave the hill and cannot stop until it is answered. Reduced to 0 hp it reforms in four nights.
> The hollow · one · killing it is a way out, not a solution, and the table should feel the difference
:::

:::npc Hoad, who has the lease | Sheep farmer, thirty years on this hill · out here nightly
WANT: To know why nothing has been taken. He can price a loss. He cannot price this.
VOICE: Short sentences. Long pauses. Points with the lamp instead of answering.
BREAK: His grandmother knew the words. He has known who to ask for eleven nights and has not asked.
LINE: If it were hungry I'd understand it. It's not hungry. It's asking.
USE: He will take you to the hollow, once, and he will not go up into it.
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle Nine Syllables | the hollow · a conversation, run as a puzzle
LOOKS: The same nine sounds, over and over, from moonrise to dawn, in a language nobody up here reads.
SOLVE: The music student's page, or Divinity's bell, or anyone who takes the trouble to write the sounds down and carry them to somebody who reads them. Then say them back, out loud, and mean it.
FAIL: Never a dead end. Every wrong answer costs a night, and there are nine nights until finals.
:::

:::item Divinity's Handbell | Wondrous, uncommon -- a locked cupboard nobody admits to
Silver, small, no clapper mark on the inside though it has plainly been rung. Rung once within earshot it answers the nine syllables, exactly, in the correct language, whether or not the ringer knows what has been said. Divinity denies owning it. Divinity has a form for its return and no form for its issue.
:::
