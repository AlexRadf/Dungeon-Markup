:::pitch
Professor Emeritus Brand gives the annual public lecture in eleven days and last year six people came, four of whom were asleep and one of whom was his wife. Draconics needs something to put on the stand: a scale, fresh cut, not shed. Or an egg, viable, whole. They have already printed a thousand posters.
!! THEME -- a heist where the mark is asleep, and the whole job is whether today is day eight or day nine.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT FIVE DAYS IN, LOOKING UP
The mine crew below the roost have counted the sleeping days for years, and they will tell you, warmly, over food, that today is day eight. They are one out and they have no idea. Above you, warm air comes off the ledge in a slow steady column, and it is going up on a nine-count, not an eight.
:::

:::beats
1. **Five days in.** The crew give you the count for free, over supper, and they are certain, and they are wrong.
2. **The approach**, under the wyverns, who are not dragons and who will kill you for the confusion.
3. **The ledge.** The warm column, and a shoulder that is figure 41 made real at forty feet across.
4. **Nine seconds at the vessel.** Count them out loud at the table. Do not roll for them.
5. **The neighbouring scale moves.** The plate said it would. Somebody has to be holding it when it does.
6. **The nest shelf**, if they want six hundred rather than two-fifty -- and something up there has already counted it. Then five days back, in the weather, with a thousand posters waiting.
:::

:::cols 2
:::place The Approach | wyverns, who are not dragons
- They fly the shelf every hour, in the same order, and they land on it and look.
- Warm air comes off the ledge in a column a hand's width from the face, the whole way up.
- They are restless today, and wyverns are never restless on a sleeping day.
:::

:::place The Ledge | the old one, and the shoulder
- Forty feet of shoulder, layered like roof slate, rising and falling on a nine-count.
- Figure 41 exactly: the free edge, the seated root, the vessel, the neighbouring scale.
- A cut scale bleeds for about nine seconds. Then it stops, and by then it has been noticed.
:::

:::place The Nest Shelf | eggs, and something has already counted them
- Eleven days from hatching, and the ledge above is the only warm place within a day's walk.
- The count is kept by the wyverns. Take one and they follow the trailer for five days, at night, in relays.
- The shelf is not guessing about which day it is, and neither is anything on it.
:::
:::

:::secrets
1. It sleeps eight days in nine. Today is day nine and nobody at the workings knows it.
2. The eggs are counted by the wyverns, not by the dragon. Take one and they follow you all the way home.
3. Brand can tell a cut scale from a shed one at ten feet. Everyone in his department cheerfully assumes he cannot.
4. The egg is viable and eleven days from hatching. The lecture is in eleven days.
5. The department already has the cooler, the stand and a thousand posters. This was decided before the job was posted.
6. The mine crew have counted the sleeping days for years and they are one out. They will tell you today is day eight.
:::

:::beats
1. **The scale bleeds, or the shelf comes up one short.** Either one, and it starts.
2. **It wakes, and it does not chase.** It waits, and then it comes down the valley behind you at its own pace.
3. **Eleven days later Brand walks on stage and lifts something into the light**, and the hall is full, and it hatches.
:::

:::puzzle Figure 41 | the ledge · nine seconds, counted out loud
LOOKS: Layered scale the size of a cart door, a free edge you can get a blade under, and a vessel beneath it.
SOLVE: Lift at (a), two inches at the seated root (b), avoid (c) absolutely, and hold (d) still while somebody else cuts. Three pairs of hands, one blade, nine seconds.
FAIL: The count runs out. It does not chase. That is very much worse, and the table should be told so.
