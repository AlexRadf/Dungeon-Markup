:::pitch
A second-year has a letter that has to be on the six o'clock mail coach out of Oakhaven, and it is ten past four, and the ford is up. Eight miles, one letter, no villain. She has eight gold, which is a term's book money, and she is holding it out already.
!! THEME -- nobody is chasing you. The only enemy tonight is the clock and your own good sense.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT IN THE STABLE YARD, TEN PAST FOUR
Rain that stopped an hour ago and is thinking about starting again. Tarn holds the grey's head and does not hand over the reins until somebody looks him in the eye. The letter goes in oilcloth, in a coat, buttoned. Eight miles that way. The yard clock in Oakhaven strikes six whether you are there or not.
:::

:::beats
1. **Ten past four, the yard.** One horse, one lamp, one letter. Whoever is not riding is running, and the road is soft.
2. **Half past, Bellow's Bottom.** The ford is over the stones and moving. Cross it (DC 12 Athletics, or Animal Handling for the horse) or go round and give up forty minutes.
3. **Five, the toll gate.** Padlocked, and Hask asleep. Ring the bell and wake the cottage, climb it (DC 11), or say what the letter is for through the window and watch him get up.
4. **Twenty past, the fen path.** Twenty minutes saved and the lamps are out. DC 12 Wisdom to hold the line; each failure is twenty minutes back the way they came.
5. **Ten to six, the Oakhaven yard.** Empty. Fresh ruts, dung still steaming, and the ostler says he went early, he had a full load.
6. **The hill road.** A mile of climb at a walk, and the letter goes up to the driver's hand from a run alongside. Nobody has to fight anybody tonight.
:::

:::cols 2

- Green, low, patient, and always exactly as far away as they were a minute ago.
- The ground gives under the heel and comes back up smelling of iron.
- A boot on a stick, driven in as a marker, pointing the way somebody else thought was right.

:::secrets
1. There is no villain in this job. If the table goes looking for one they will find a wet night and a slow horse.
2. The coach can leave early. It has twice this month. Assume ten to six and let them find that out at the yard.
3. The ford is passable and the long way round is forty minutes. Both are true, and choosing is the whole adventure.
4. Hask will open the gate at once, awake, for anybody who says what the letter is for. The bell is the slow way in.
5. The marsh lamps cannot hurt anyone. They can cost twenty minutes each, and twenty minutes is everything tonight.
6. The hill road out of Oakhaven climbs for a mile and the coach walks it. That is the second chance, and it is real.
7. The driver will take a letter handed up to him at a walk. He has done it before. He will not stop, and he will not need to.
8. Ren is at the yard gate at six anyway, having walked the eight miles in the dark, because she could not sit still.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- The clock. Six o'clock, or ten to. Count it down out loud in ten-minute steps and let them hear it going.
- One horse, and it is Tarn's, and it is lent on trust. Ride it into the fen and it is lamed, and that is its own small tragedy.
- Every shortcut here is a real trade: forty minutes of road against a flooded ford, twenty against the lamps.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Make the yard by six. The straight version, and it is achievable if they stop arguing at the ford.
- Catch it on the hill road, at a walk, and hand the letter up. Nothing has to stop for this to work.
- Miss it entirely, and find the down coach at eight from the Cardell side, which arrives a day late and still beats the ship.
:::

:::roll d6 | THE ROAD
1 The rain starts again, properly.
2 A cart, going the wrong way, wants to talk.
3 The lamp blows out. Relighting it is four minutes.
4 The grey throws a shoe -- or nearly, and they must decide.
5 A milestone, and the mile is longer than the last one.
6 Somebody in the fen is singing. It is a shepherd. He knows the path.

:::valve
If it's dragging: the ostler at the yard remembers the driver saying he'd water the horses at the top of the hill.
If it's going fast: they make it by ten to, and Ren is at the gate, soaked, having walked the eight miles behind them.
Cliffhanger: a fortnight later, a letter comes back up the road addressed to the party by name, from Cardell, from somebody who sailed.
