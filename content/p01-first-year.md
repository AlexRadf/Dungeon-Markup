:::pitch
Welcome to Eldoria. You are a first-year here, and so is your character, and neither of you knows where anything is. That is the correct amount to know.
!! You do not have to learn the game. You have to say what you want to do, and ask.
:::

:::box dark | THE WHOLE GAME, IN FOUR LINES
**One.** Somebody — the GM — describes where you are and what is happening.

**Two.** You say what your character does about it. In your own words. *I go and look behind the curtain.*

**Three.** If it might not work, you roll a twenty-sided die to find out.

**Four.** The GM says what happens next, and you go round again.

That is it. Everything else in this pack is detail on line two and line three.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | YOU CANNOT GET THIS WRONG
There is no losing. There is no wrong move, no wasted turn, and nothing you can say that breaks it.

You do not need to know a single rule to play tonight. Everyone else at the table knows some, between them, and none of them knows all of them either. That is normal and permanent.

If you forget your character's name, ask. If you miss what happened, ask. Everybody does this every week.
:::

:::box gold | THE ONE SENTENCE THAT ALWAYS WORKS
> **"I'd like to try to ______. Can I?"**

Fill in anything at all. Climb it, charm it, steal it, set fire to it, ask it a question.

The GM will say yes, or say roll, or tell you what is stopping you. You never have to know which of those it was going to be before you say it.
:::
:::

### Your character, in six numbers

You have a sheet with six scores on it. Ignore where they came from. Each one is really a question, and the only thing that matters is that a bigger number means you are better at answering it.

| | | THE QUESTION IT ANSWERS | SO YOU USE IT FOR |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STR** | Strength | *Can I move it?* | Doors, grapples, hitting things, carrying your friend |
| **DEX** | Dexterity | *Can I do it neatly?* | Sneaking, balancing, picking pockets and locks, bows |
| **CON** | Constitution | *Can I take it?* | Poison, cold, exhaustion, staying upright |
| **INT** | Intelligence | *Do I already know this?* | Books, magic, puzzles, working things out |
| **WIS** | Wisdom | *Do I notice, and do I believe them?* | Spotting things, reading people, first aid |
| **CHA** | Charisma | *Can I talk them round?* | Persuading, lying, threatening, performing |

**When you do not know which one to use, say what you are doing and let the GM pick.** That is genuinely part of their job, and they would rather choose than watch you look for the right word.

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ROLLING THE DIE
Take the **d20** — the big one with twenty faces. Roll it. Add the small number written next to the thing you are doing. Say the total out loud.

Higher is better. The GM has a target in mind and will tell you whether you cleared it.

**You will fail about a third of the time, and that is the good part.** A story where everything works is a story where nothing happens. Some of the best things that occur tonight will be things that went wrong.
:::

:::box plain | TWO DICE INSTEAD OF ONE
Sometimes the GM says roll with **advantage**. Roll two d20s and use the better one. Something is helping you: good footing, a friend holding the ladder, a lie they already half believe.

Sometimes it is **disadvantage**. Roll two and use the worse one. Something is against you — the dark, a bad shoulder, the fact that you have already tried this once.

That is the whole of it. Nothing to add up.
:::
:::

### When it is your turn in a fight

Fights are just the same game with an order to it. When it comes round to you, you get **three things**, and you can do them in any order:

:::cols 2
:::box plain | MOVE
Walk, run, climb, back off, get behind something. Roughly six squares, and you can split it around the rest of your turn — some before, some after.
:::

:::box plain | DO ONE THING
Swing at somebody. Cast a spell. Shove them into the pond. Yank the lever. Grab your friend and pull. **Anything you can describe counts as a thing** — ask, and it will be allowed or it will be a roll.
:::
:::

And **say something**, always, free of charge — shout a warning, make a threat, ask a question. Talking never costs you your turn.

:::box gold | IF YOU GO DOWN, YOU ARE ALMOST CERTAINLY NOT DEAD
At zero hit points you fall over, unconscious, and start rolling a d20 each turn to see how you do. **Ten or more is good.** Any healing at all, of any amount, and you are back up.

Your friends will come and get you, because that is what the game is about. Dying in your first session takes a truly determined effort and quite a lot of bad luck.
:::

### Five questions for when you have no idea what to do

:::beats
1. **"What do I see?"** Ask the GM to describe the room again. Say what catches your eye. Half the time the answer to the puzzle is in the second description.
2. **"What would my character know about this?"** This is the most useful sentence in the game. Your character has lived here and you have not. The GM will hand you facts you did not know you had.
3. **"What is my character afraid of, here?"** Play that. Fear makes better choices than tactics do, and other people at the table can react to it.
4. **"What would be the most fun to watch?"** Not the safest. Not the cleverest. If two options are equal, pick the one you would rather see happen.
5. **"What do you lot think?"** Ask the others. This is a group game and nobody is scoring you individually.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | THINGS NEW PLAYERS WORRY ABOUT
**"I'll slow everyone down."** You will take longer on your turn than they do for about three sessions. Nobody minds. They all did it.

**"I don't know the rules."** Neither do they. Look at the person who has played longest and count how often they look something up.

**"My idea is stupid."** Say it anyway. Stupid ideas are the entire history of this hobby, and this campus in particular.

**"I'm not good at voices."** Then do not do voices. *He says he wants us to leave* is completely valid play, forever.

**"I'll ruin it for everyone."** The only way to do that is to stop taking part. Everything else is just what happened.
:::

:::box dark | SIX THINGS YOU CAN SAY, WORD FOR WORD
**"I'd like to try to ______. What do I roll?"**

**"What would my character know about that?"**

**"Can I have a closer look at the ______?"**

**"I want to talk them round — can I roll for that?"**

**"I'm not sure what my options are. What could I do?"**

**"I'd rather we skipped that bit."** — always allowed, no reason needed, and everyone will simply move on.
:::
:::

:::box red | THE ONLY ETIQUETTE THAT MATTERS
Let other people finish their turn and their sentences. Take an interest when it is not your go — the best thing that happens tonight will probably happen to somebody else. Say yes to other players' ideas more than you say no to them.

And ask questions constantly. A table where somebody asks a question every five minutes is a table that is going well.
:::

:::valve
Rolled badly: Nothing is over. Ask what it looks like when it goes wrong, and go from there.
Lost the thread: Say so, out loud, right away. Everyone else is quietly a bit lost too.
Nothing to do: Ask another player what their character is doing, and do it with them.
Bored: Go and touch something you were told not to touch. It has never once failed.
:::
