:::box dark | THREE THINGS BEFORE YOU ASK ANYBODY FOR A d20
**Know what failure costs.** If nothing changes when they fail, there is no roll — they simply do it. A locked door with nothing behind the failure is an open door with a delay in front of it.

**One roll, not five.** A party searching a room is one check by the best-placed character. Everybody else **Helps**, which is advantage, not another die.

**Ask how.** *How are you doing that?* settles the ability, settles the DC, and about half the time settles that there was never a roll to make.
:::

:::cols 2
| DC | HOW HARD | ON THIS CAMPUS |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Very easy | Reading a notice pinned at eye height |
| 10 | Easy | Talking past a first-year on the door |
| 15 | Medium | The Registrar's desk drawer, quietly |
| 20 | Hard | The Chemistry facade, in the wet |
| 25 | Very hard | A seal that survives being inspected |
| 30 | Nearly impossible | Out-arguing the Bursar about fees |

| ABILITY | SKILLS | WHAT IT ASKS |
|---|---|---|
| STR | Athletics | Can I move it or hold on? |
| DEX | Acrobatics · Sleight of Hand · Stealth | Neatly, without falling? |
| CON | *saves only* | Can I keep going? |
| INT | Arcana · History · Investigation · Nature · Religion | Do I know it, or work it out? |
| WIS | Animal Handling · Insight · Medicine · Perception · Survival | Do I notice, and believe them? |
| CHA | Deception · Intimidation · Performance · Persuasion | Will they do what I want? |
:::

**No idea what the DC is? It is 15.** Ten if they have a good angle on it, twenty if they are pushing their luck.

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ADVANTAGE, DISADVANTAGE, PASSIVE
**Advantage** is 2d20, keep the higher; **disadvantage** is 2d20, keep the lower. They do not stack — three good reasons is still one advantage — and one of each cancels the whole lot, however many there are of either.

**Passive Perception is 10 + the modifier**, and it is never rolled. It is what the room does to them; a Perception check is what they do to the room.

:::

:::box plain | TALKING TO PEOPLE
Set the attitude first — **hostile, indifferent, friendly** — and a check moves it one step. Nobody goes from hostile to helpful on one roll, and a natural 20 on Persuasion is not a spell.

**Insight reads intent, not truth.** *He believes what he is telling you* is a real answer and usually the better one.

Give every NPC a want before they open their mouth, even if the want is *to be left alone*.
:::
:::

| THE THING | THE ANSWER |
|---|---|
| Jumping | Long is your STR score in feet with a 10 ft run-up, half that standing; high is 3 + your STR modifier |
| Climbing, swimming, difficult terrain | Every foot of it costs two |
| Falling | 1d6 per 10 ft, maximum 20d6, and you land prone |
| Holding your breath | 1 + CON modifier minutes, minimum 30 seconds. Standing up from prone costs half your speed |
| A torch | 20 ft bright, 20 ft dim beyond it — dim light is disadvantage on Perception, and you cannot hide from anything that can plainly see you |

### The turn, and the body

:::box dark | ONE TURN IS THREE THINGS
**Move** up to your speed, in as many pieces as you like. **One action.** **One bonus action, only if something gives you one.** Between your turns, **one reaction** — usually an opportunity attack when somebody leaves your reach, never against somebody who Disengaged.
:::

:::cols 2
| ACTION | WHAT IT DOES |
|---|---|
| Attack | One attack; more only if a feature says so |
| Cast a Spell | As written. One levelled spell a turn |
| Dash | Move your speed again |
| Disengage | Your movement provokes nothing this turn |
| Dodge | Attacks on you have disadvantage, your DEX saves advantage |
| Help | Advantage on an ally's check, or their next attack within 5 ft |
| Hide | Stealth against passive Perception, if there is cover to use |
| Ready | Name a trigger and a response; it spends your reaction |
| Search | Look for one specific thing |
| Use an Object | Pull it, uncork it, throw it |
| Grapple or Shove | In place of one attack: Athletics vs Athletics or Acrobatics |
| Improvise | Everything else. Say yes and pick an ability |

| CONDITION | WHAT IT DOES |
|---|---|
| Blinded | Attacks on it advantage, its own disadvantage |
| Charmed | Cannot attack the charmer; charmer has social adv. |
| Deafened | Fails anything that needs hearing |
| Frightened | Disadv. while the source is in sight; cannot close |
| Grappled | Speed 0; ends if the grappler is downed |
| Incapacitated | No actions and no reactions |
| Invisible | Attacks on it disadvantage, its own advantage |
| Paralysed | Incapacitated; hits within 5 ft are critical |
| Poisoned | Disadv. on attacks and ability checks |
| Prone | Crawls at half speed; melee adv., ranged disadv. |
| Restrained | Speed 0; attacks adv., its own and DEX saves disadv. |
| Stunned | Incapacitated; auto-fails STR and DEX saves |
| Unconscious | Prone, drops all; hits within 5 ft are critical |
| Exhaustion 1–6 | Disadv. checks · half speed · disadv. attacks and saves · half hp · speed 0 · dead |
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | AT 0 HIT POINTS
Down, unconscious, and rolling. At the start of each of your turns roll a **d20**: **10 or more is a success, 9 or less a failure.** Three successes and you are stable, three failures and you are dead. A natural **20** puts you back up on 1 hp; a natural **1** counts as two failures.

Damage taken while down costs a failure — **two if it was a critical, and every hit from within 5 ft is a critical.** Any healing at all, of any amount, puts you up.

**Stabilising:** a DC 10 Medicine check as an action, or a healer's kit — they stay unconscious, but stop dying. **Dead outright:** damage left over after you hit 0 that equals your hit point maximum.
:::

:::box plain | THE REST OF THE NUMBERS
**Critical hit:** roll the damage dice twice, add the modifier once. Only ever on a natural 20 on an attack roll.

**Cover:** half is **+2** to AC and DEX saves, three-quarters **+5**, total cannot be targeted.

**Short rest:** one hour, spending Hit Dice to heal one at a time. **Long rest:** eight hours, all hit points back and half your Hit Dice, once in any 24 hours.

**Surprise** is not a round. Anybody surprised does nothing on their first turn and cannot react until it ends. **Initiative** is a DEX check by another name: roll once, keep the order, never re-roll mid-fight.
:::
:::

Say **what it smells of**, **what is broken**, and **who left in a hurry**. That is a location. The party build the rest themselves and then believe they found it there.

### When it goes off the rails

:::box dark | THEY HAVE GONE SOMEWHERE YOU DID NOT PREPARE. FIVE QUESTIONS, IN ORDER
**1. What did they come here for?** It is here — or the reason it is not is more interesting than the thing was.

**2. Who is already in the room?** Somebody always is. Roll the d20 on the previous panel.

**3. What does that person want?** One want, one sentence, and it is not the party's want.

**4. What is the complication?** Roll the d10. It does not have to be a fight, and usually should not be.

**5. What are the two ways out?** Name both. One of them leads somewhere you have actually prepped.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box gold | THE THREE RULES OF SAYING YES
**Say yes, or roll.** Never a flat no. If the answer is no, it is because a die said so, or because they can see for themselves why.

**Fail forward.** They get in, but loudly. They find it, but it is broken. They arrive, but late. No single check ends a session.

**Steal your own prep.** The corridor they invented is the corridor you drew, with a different door on it. Nobody at this table has read your notes, and nobody ever will.
:::

:::valve
Stalled: Somebody knocks. Ask a player who it is, and use their answer.
Dithering: Put a clock on the table and tick it where they can see it.
Too easy: It works — and somebody heard it work.
Too hard: An NPC does one competent thing. They are allowed to help.
Dragging: Cut. You may say *twenty minutes later* out loud and nobody minds.
Lost: Hand them the answer through somebody irritated to be handing it over.
:::
:::

**Those are the numbers this book's own stat blocks sit on** — near enough for a monster you invented in the middle of a sentence. At **level 1–2**, one CR 1/2 per player is a real fight and one CR 2 is a boss. Numbers hurt more than size does, because they all act: bring the second wave in a round late rather than all at once.

:::box red | HOW TO END A SESSION YOU HAVE LOST CONTROL OF
Stop where somebody has just decided something, not where the fight ended. Say what the consequence is going to be, and let them wait a week for it.

Then ask the table two questions out loud and write the answers on the notes sheet: **what are you doing next?** and **who do you still owe?** Whatever they say is next week's prep. It is better than anything you would have invented, and it took ninety seconds.
:::
