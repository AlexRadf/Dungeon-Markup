Every hex on the map has an entry, and the entry is the whole adventure: what shows from the hex line, why a party charters it, what is dangerous, what is worth carrying home, and which hex it points at next. Nothing here is a plot -- run the hex they chartered and let the **LEADS** line do the pulling.

## Ring one · one day out and back · levels 1–2

0101 · 0201 · 0102 · 0202 · 0203. Sheep country and the first three miles of a mountain nobody has climbed since 1189. Nothing in ring one is trying to kill anybody; most of it will anyway, if a first level is careless with a ford.

:::cols 2
:::roll d8 | RING ONE -- WHAT FINDS YOU
1 A shepherd, four dogs, and eighty sheep in your way for a whole watch.
2 Wolves, 2d4, thin, following rather than hunting.
3 Third-years from Eldoria, chartered for the next hex over, cheerful and a liability.
4 Stirges, 1d4+2, out of a crack in the ground you did not see.
5 A crow that lands on the drawn map and will not be shifted.
6 Bandits, 1d4+1, who want the lamp oil and not a fight.
7 A giant badger in a hole, with something brass in the spoil.
8 Weather off the Teeth. Lose the watch or push at DC 13 CON.
:::

:::hex 0101 | The First Tooth | Mountain -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: The westmost peak of the Cold Teeth, and a goat track up it that somebody cut with tools.
HOOK: There is a horse standing in the pass. It has been standing there, in the cold, since before anyone's grandmother.
RISK: Scree, DC 12 or 2d6 and a lost watch. The cold is the monster: one exhaustion per watch above the snow line without shelter.
TAKE: The horse's satchel: 1189 survey paper, dry, and eleven names on the muster, one of them still being paid.
LEADS: The muster names the stair at 0201 and a hall east called Marrowstone.
:::

:::hex 0201 | The Snowmelt Stair | Mountain -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: Four hundred cut steps going up into cloud, each twice the rise a person would cut.
HOOK: Meltwater comes down the stair all summer, and Kettlewick's mill runs on it. This year it stopped for nine days, then came back brown.
RISK: What cut the stair still uses it -- an **ogre**, old, lame, incurious about a party that stands still. A fight here is a fall.
TAKE: Whatever it took off the last party -- 200 gp of it, and a lamp that will not blow out.
LEADS: The stair goes over the ridge and comes down at 0202, which is the only crossing until 0301.
:::

:::hex 0102 | The Kettle | Hills -- ring 1 -- half a day
SEE: The bald hill the town is named for, steaming on cold mornings, terraced by somebody a long time ago.
HOOK: The spring has gone bitter this month and the town drinks from it. Slake will pay ring-one rate to know why, twice if it is fixed.
RISK: Stirges in the terrace cracks, 1d4+2. The vent scalds: 2d6 fire if the ground shifts under a search.
TAKE: The survey's first benchmark, a brass disc set in the rock, still true. And the spring, if they clear the dead thing out of it.
LEADS: The disc is stamped with a bearing. It points at 0202, and past it.
:::

:::hex 0202 | The Listening Cairn | Mountain -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: A cairn on the saddle, man-high, and the wind coming off it wrong -- shaped, as if it had gone through a mouth.
HOOK: Downwind of the cairn the wind says names. Some of them are names of people in Kettlewick. One of them is not a person yet.
RISK: **Twelvetrees**, the hermit who tends it, who will not be moved and is far better at this country than the party. Hostile only if the cairn is opened.
TAKE: Under the cap stone, the survey's second mark, and a bundle of charter numbers going up to eleven.
LEADS: Twelvetrees walked to the far pass at 0703 once, and will say what turned him back, for food.
:::

:::hex 0203 | Near Acre | Heath -- ring 1 -- half a day
SEE: Gorse, grazing, and a fallen milestone in the grass with the old road's distances still cut in it.
HOOK: The first hex every party files, and the one Slake offers a party she does not rate. The road under the heath goes somewhere.
RISK: Almost none, and that is the lesson. A boar, a bad ford, a shepherd with a grievance and a good arm.
TAKE: 50 gp of tariff and a place to set the party's first mark, which is worth more than the money.
LEADS: The milestone reads EIGHTEEN MILES to a hall nobody in town has heard of -- three hexes east, at 0403.
:::
:::

## Ring one, continued

0303 · 0104 · 0204 · 0105 · 0205 -- the wood's edge and the southern heath. Every one of these is a day's walk, and every one of them is somebody's first night at this table.

:::cols 2

:::hex 0303 | The Eaves | Forest -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: Where the heath stops and the wood starts, in a line, like a wall someone built.
HOOK: The tree line is two hundred yards nearer town than the 1189 survey drew it, and the charcoal burners agree it moved this spring.
RISK: The burners: eight of them, armed, and certain the party is here to move them on. **Wolves**, 2d4, after dark, at the edge of the firelight.
TAKE: A burner's forty-year memory of the wood, sold cheap, and a fresh stump ring that counts wrong.
LEADS: The burners will not go east of 0304. They will say the word *line* and change the subject.
:::

:::hex 0104 | Sixty-One Stones | Heath -- ring 1 -- half a day
SEE: A field of small standing stones, none taller than a child, in no pattern that resolves from the ground.
HOOK: They come to sixty-one on the way out and sixty-two on the way back. Slake wants the number settled and will pay for a drawn plan.
RISK: Nothing attacks. Anybody who counts alone, twice, must make a DC 12 WIS save or walk the field until somebody says their name.
TAKE: The extra stone is a mark, laid flat, face down. The face is a map of this hex with two things drawn on it that are not here yet.
LEADS: The drawn plan, laid over the Ledger's map, is the whole line -- but only at 0504 does anyone see that.
:::

:::hex 0204 | The Fold | Heath -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: A sheepfold with no sheep in it, a good fire, and four horses that are better than the party's.
HOOK: Third-years, chartered for 0305 and camped short of it, are charging a toll to cross a fold that is not theirs.
RISK: Five level 2 rivals who will not draw first and will absolutely draw second. Losing here costs money and standing, not lives.
TAKE: Their charter, if they can be shamed out of it, is a free hex at ring two rate. Their contempt otherwise, all campaign.
LEADS: They have been to 0305 and turned back at the fourth bridge, and they will sell why for the toll they wanted.
:::

:::hex 0105 | The Near Fen | Marsh -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: Where the ground stops being reliable. Alder, standing water, and a punt chained to a post on the near bank.
HOOK: The chain is Kettlewick's, the punt is not, and the man who owned the punt was filed missing in the spring.
RISK: Three watches to cross, DC 14 not to be turned around. Leeches; then what has been feeding the leeches.
TAKE: The punt, which halves every fen crossing in ring one and two for the rest of the campaign. The man, in the reeds, still holding his charter.
LEADS: His charter is numbered for 0305 and countersigned by a department nobody at the counter recognises.
:::

:::hex 0205 | The Burnt Row | Heath -- ring 1 -- one day
SEE: Nine cottage footings in a row on open heath, burnt down to the stone, with grass grown over everything but the burn.
HOOK: They went in one night in 1189, all nine, and nothing between them or beyond them caught. Fire does not do that.
RISK: Nothing living. Anyone who sleeps inside a footing wakes at the same moment with the same dream of a straight line coming west.
TAKE: A cellar under the fourth house, unburnt, with a family's coin, 180 gp, and a stone in the middle of the floor that was set, not dropped.
LEADS: The burn line runs dead east: 0305, 0405, and on out of ring two towards the waste at 0704.
:::

:::npc Twelvetrees | Human · hermit of the Listening Cairn · has not slept indoors since 1189
WANT: The cairn tended, and nobody's hands on it but his. He will not say whose orders.
VOICE: A long pause, then a whole paragraph, then nothing at all for a week.
BREAK: A survey stone, shown to him. He stands up. He asks who is holding the west now.
LINE: You are the eleventh lot. Nine turned back. Ask me about the tenth.
USE: The best guide in rings one and two -- cancels the Lost check. Takes food, never coin, and will not cross east of 0304.
:::

:::box plain | RUNNING A RING-ONE NIGHT
Two watches out, one to open the hex, two home, and the whistle at dusk. That is the whole clock, and it is the tutorial: the party learns that daylight, not hit points, is what ends a session.

Do not put anything in ring one that a first level cannot walk away from. Put in things they cannot **explain** -- the moved tree line, the extra stone, the burn that stopped -- and let them file it and be paid, and lie awake about it.
:::
:::

## Ring two · two days out · levels 3–5

0301 · 0401 · 0302 · 0402 · 0403. The wood proper and the first place the old survey left furniture. A party that has not reclaimed **Marrowstone Hall at 0403** is spending two of its four watches walking.

:::cols 2
:::roll d8 | RING TWO -- WHAT FINDS YOU
1 **Dire wolves**, 1d4+1, working the party the way the party works a hex.
2 A stag the size of a cart horse, with a surveyor's chain wound in its antlers.
3 **Harpies**, 1d3+1, on the water, singing something in a clerk's flat voice.
4 A rival party's mark, freshly set, in the hex you chartered. Dated tomorrow.
5 **Ghouls**, 1d4+2, out of the peat, dressed for 1189.
6 An **ogre** or **hill giant** working east along the burn line, not hunting, walking.
7 Fog for two watches. Every check to navigate is at disadvantage and something is counting your party.
8 A warden of the line, on a horse, who asks to see a mark and rides off if shown one.
:::

:::hex 0301 | The Tooth That Fell | Mountain -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A peak with its north face missing, and half a mountain lying in the valley in pieces the size of houses.
HOOK: It came down in 1189, the same season as the Burn, and the rubble is full of cut stone that was never part of a mountain.
RISK: Live scree, DC 14 or 3d6 and buried. Whatever survived under there has been eating what the rockfall traps.
TAKE: Cut stone by the ton, and one intact chamber of it: a survey station with its instruments still on the bench.
LEADS: The instruments are stamped with a quarry mark. The quarry is at 0401.
:::

:::hex 0401 | The Quarry of Marks | Hills -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A worked chalk quarry, benches cut clean, blanks stacked and squared, and swept.
HOOK: It is stocked, sharp and tidy, and no one has worked it in three hundred years. Every survey stone in the west came out of this hole.
RISK: The sweeping. Something keeps this yard, works at night, and does not want the stack disturbed by anybody without a charter number.
TAKE: **Blank marks.** Forty pounds each, and the reason a hex pays double. A party can carry two, three if somebody suffers.
LEADS: The tally board lists every mark cut and where it went. Four are down as UNSET, at 0504, 0604, 0703 and 0704.
:::

:::hex 0302 | Hollowbeck | Forest -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A mile of dead standing trees, no bark, no fall, all of them leaning the same few degrees east.
HOOK: The beck that named it runs clear and the wood beside it is a hundred years dead, and both facts are the same age.
RISK: **Wolves**, 3d4, denned in the hollow trunks, and a floor of dead root that gives at DC 13.
TAKE: Dead-dry timber worth nothing and burning like nothing else; two lengths make a torch that lasts a whole watch.
LEADS: Everything leans towards 0402, and in 0402 nothing leans at all.
:::

:::hex 0402 | The Canopy | Forest -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: Old wood, high crown, and no undergrowth at all -- a floor swept bare, a hundred feet under the branches.
HOOK: Everything alive here lives up. Birds, cats, deer in the crooks of branches, and a rope camp forty feet up with a ladder pulled after it.
RISK: Whatever swept the floor still sweeps it, nightly, and is never seen from above. A character on the ground after dark is a character rolling saves.
TAKE: The camp: a survey party's, sixty years newer than 1189, with a filed hex map of the whole ring two and a name signed to it.
LEADS: The signature is Slake's. Ask her about it and she says: *that is my mother's hand,* and nothing else, ever.
:::

:::hex 0403 | Marrowstone Hall | Built -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A long chalk-block hall on a hill with a walled yard, a well, a roof mostly on, and a fire lit in it most nights.
HOOK: The 1189 forward station. Reclaim it and it is the party's: **a hex of travel comes off every ring three and four charter.**
RISK: Whoever lights the fire. Not the survey and not an animal -- a household of eight, three generations, who have kept the hall since their great-grandmother deserted the survey to do it.
TAKE: The hall itself, its well, its ledger of every party that passed through, and the household as hirelings, if they are handled like people.
LEADS: The hall's ledger has entries after 1189. The last one is dated eleven years ago and it is a supply order for 0704.
:::
:::

## Ring two, continued

0304 · 0404 · 0305 · 0405 · 0503 -- the line, the fen and the first water. This is the half of ring two where the campaign stops being about money.

:::cols 2

:::hex 0304 | The Deer Line | Forest -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A wall of skulls, deer and boar and wolf, set on posts in a line running north to south for the whole width of the hex.
HOOK: Nothing with four legs crosses it eastward. Not one animal. The burners at 0303 built it and cannot say who told them how.
RISK: Cross it and be counted: DC 14 WIS or every animal in ring two knows where the party is until they cross back.
TAKE: The posts are set on a line of small buried stones, spaced exactly as the map's hex edges are spaced. That is the first hard proof.
LEADS: North the line runs to 0303 and 0202; south, into the fen at 0404, where the peat kept the paper.
:::

:::hex 0404 | The Paper Fen | Marsh -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: Black peat, standing water, and pages -- hundreds of them -- lying on the surface of a bog, dry.
HOOK: The fen is giving back the 1189 filings, one storm at a time. Everything the old survey wrote is coming up out of the ground.
RISK: Three watches to cross and the footing takes people. What the peat kept, it kept whole: **ghouls**, 2d4, in survey grey.
TAKE: Filings. Each armful is worth a ring-two tariff to Slake, and one sheet in ten describes a hex that is not on any map.
LEADS: The filings for 0504 are all there, and every one of them has the same line struck through it in a different hand.
:::

:::hex 0305 | Ninebridges | Marsh -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: A causeway across the deep fen on nine bridges, of which four still take weight, laid on cut stone.
HOOK: The only dry road east through the fen, and the fastest way to the whole southern half of the map -- if the bridges hold.
RISK: The fourth bridge. Something under it comes up when weight goes on, and the third-years at 0204 turned back rather than learn what.
TAKE: Repair the four and the causeway is a road: no Lost check through 0305 or 0405 for anyone, ever again.
LEADS: The causeway was built to carry something heavy east, and it was built by the yard at 0401.
:::

:::hex 0405 | The Still | Marsh -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: Open fen water, a mile of it, that does not ripple. Not in wind, not when a stone goes in.
HOOK: The burn line from 0205 comes east and stops at this water, and the water is where the fire stopped.
RISK: Sound does not carry over it. A party that separates on the Still cannot call to each other, and something in it counts on that.
TAKE: Standing in the water, invisible from any bank, a mark -- upright, unbroken, and warm to the hand in a cold fen.
LEADS: Warm marks are marks under load. The cold ones are at 0504 and 0604.
:::

:::hex 0503 | The Drown, First Tarn | Water -- ring 2 -- two days
SEE: The first of the four waters, black and deep and cold, with the old road going down its western bank and not coming up the eastern.
HOOK: There is a boat on it, well out, with four sets of oars shipped and nobody aboard, and it has not moved in living memory.
RISK: Swimming here is dying here. Cold water, DC 13 CON per watch, and the boat is not the deepest thing in this tarn.
TAKE: In the boat: four sets of kit, dry, four charter numbers, and a mark laid along the keel like ballast, never set.
LEADS: The road under the water carries on east and surfaces at the weir in 0504.
:::

:::item A blank mark | Survey stone -- cut at 0401, forty pounds
Chalk, squared, unlettered. Set one upright in a hex, cut the coordinate into the face and file it, and the hex pays double and never needs a Lost check again.

Carrying one is 40 lb and worth every ounce: a mark in hand turns the warden at 0601 aside, opens the pass at 0703, and buys anything the sorter at 0604 has. There are four unset marks somewhere on this map and the quarry board knows exactly where.
:::

:::npc Aud Marrowstone | Human · fourth generation of a deserter's household · 0403
WANT: The hall kept, the roof on, and her family not marched back to Eldoria in irons.
VOICE: Careful, formal, three hundred years out of date. Calls every party *the relief*.
BREAK: The word *desertion*. Her great-grandmother walked off the survey and she has heard it her whole life.
LINE: We did not abandon the post. We are the post. You are extremely late.
USE: Hand her a charter and the hall is a forward camp -- one hex off every ring three and four walk.
:::
:::

## Ring three · three to four days out · levels 6–8

0501 · 0601 · 0502 · 0602 · 0603. Deep wood and deep water. Ring three is where a party stops filing hexes and starts reading them: the marks, the line, and the fact that all of this was surveyed once already, by people who are not finished.

:::cols 2
:::roll d8 | RING THREE -- WHAT FINDS YOU
1 **Trolls**, 1d3, working the water margin, and they know the crossings.
2 A **wyvern** off the Teeth, hunting the open hills for two watches.
3 A warden of the line: an armoured rider, unrelenting, who kills the unmarked and salutes the marked.
4 **Revenants**, 1d4, in survey grey, still walking their assigned hex, still counting.
5 A **hill giant** carrying a survey stone the way a person carries a fence post, going east.
6 The wood moves in the night. Camp is one hex from where it was pitched. Nothing else happens.
7 A **banshee** at a barrow, who was on the muster at 0101 and would like to hear her name said right.
8 Rival surveyors, six, chartered by nobody, taking marks *out* of the ground.
:::

:::hex 0501 | The Sixty-Year Camp | Forest -- ring 3 -- three days
SEE: A camp laid out exactly the way Eldoria teaches it: fire, spoil pit, lines, drawing board east-facing.
HOOK: It is sixty years old and the party has never seen anyone but themselves lay a camp like this.
RISK: Nothing hostile. The horror is procedural: the drawing board carries tonight's hex, half drawn, in a hand a player will recognise as their own character's.
TAKE: The half-drawn sheet finishes any one hex in ring three without entering it. Once. Slake pays and asks no questions and does not look up.
LEADS: The sheet's margin note: *the wood is not where we left it -- see 0603.*
:::

:::hex 0601 | The Warden's Ride | Forest -- ring 3 -- four days
SEE: A ride cut dead straight through old forest, twelve feet wide, running north to south, with no stump older than a season.
HOOK: Something keeps this ride clear and rides it. Parties that stand aside are passed. Parties that stand in it are ridden down.
RISK: **The warden.** Armour, lance, no face under the helm, and a standing order three hundred years old to admit nobody without a mark.
TAKE: Show a mark from 0401 and the warden dismounts, hands over its tally, and asks -- in a voice like a lock turning -- for relief.
LEADS: The tally lists every unset mark in the west, and the four it names are the four the quarry board named.
:::

:::hex 0502 | The Deep Tarn | Water -- ring 3 -- three days
SEE: The second water, a mile across, and the old road going into it at a shallow angle and still visible for two hundred feet down.
HOOK: The road is intact under there, cambered and kerbed, and it is going somewhere the map does not show.
RISK: Cold, dark, pressure, and the thing that has kept the road clear of weed. Every watch below is a death saving throw waiting for a reason.
TAKE: The drowned station on the road at the bottom: sealed, dry, and holding four marks and the only complete 1189 map of ring four.
LEADS: That map has one hex on it that the party's map does not. It is drawn at 0705, and the paper there is scraped through.
:::

:::hex 0602 | The Long Barrow | Forest -- ring 3 -- four days
SEE: Eighty graves in a bank of turf, cut in one week, and every one of them opened from the inside.
HOOK: The muster from 0101 is eighty-one names. Eighty are buried here. The eighty-first is still being paid by the Bursar's office.
RISK: They are not in the graves and they are not hostile until named wrong. Say a name off the muster incorrectly and that one comes.
TAKE: The barrow's foot-stone is a mark, set, holding, and carrying eighty names as its inscription. It is the strongest thing on the line.
LEADS: The eighty-first name is on the payroll in Kettlewick, drawing a stipend at an address in 0605.
:::

:::hex 0603 | The Walking Wood | Forest -- ring 3 -- three days
SEE: Old wood, wrong ground: roots standing proud of soil that has not been under them long.
HOOK: This hex was a mile further east when the first-years filed 0303, and the burners have been saying so for two generations.
RISK: Not treants and nothing to fight. Camp here and the hex moves with the party inside it -- they wake in the right wood and the wrong hex, one west.
TAKE: What the wood is walking away from, exposed where the roots pulled up: a mark, snapped off at the ground, and the stump is warm.
LEADS: The wood is going west because the break is east. The break is at 0504.
:::
:::

## Ring three, continued

0504 · 0604 · 0505 · 0605 · 0703 -- the weir, the spoil, the drain, the assay house and the pass. A party that files all five of these knows what the Grey Survey is, whether or not you ever tell them.

:::cols 2

:::hex 0504 | The Weir of Marks | Water -- ring 3 -- three days
SEE: A line of survey stones crossing the third water, twenty feet apart, standing in the current like a weir. One is broken.
HOOK: This is where the campaign changes. From the water, in a line, the marks run north out of sight and south out of sight, and they are all the same stone.
RISK: The break. Water goes through it wrong -- upstream, in a channel the width of a mark -- and what comes through with it arrives 1d4 rounds after anyone touches the stump.
TAKE: Setting a blank here closes the break. Slake pays ring-three double, Grasp countersigns without a word, and the west gets quieter for a season.
LEADS: North the line runs to the pass at 0703. South it runs to 0505, and past that it is not a line any more.
:::

:::hex 0604 | The Spoil | Hills -- ring 3 -- four days
SEE: Grey hills that are not hills: three hundred years of quarry spoil, tipped, and something has been working through it from underneath.
HOOK: The tips are being sorted. Cut stone to one side, waste to the other, in the dark, by something with no reason to be tidy.
RISK: What sorts. It is strong, patient, and has been told to find every mark ever cut and it is nearly finished.
TAKE: An unset mark, one of the quarry board's four, at the bottom of a sorted stack -- and the sorter will let it go for a full one in trade.
LEADS: Whatever gave that order gave it from 0704, and the sorter will point east with a hand the size of a door.
:::

:::hex 0505 | The Mouth | Water -- ring 3 -- three days
SEE: The last water in the chain, going into the ground -- a slow, silent, forty-foot drain in open fen, and nothing coming out anywhere.
HOOK: Everything the four waters carry goes down here, and none of it has ever been found downstream, because there is no downstream.
RISK: The pull is DC 15 within a hundred feet by boat, DC 17 in the water. Down is survivable. Down is also where the campaign's bad news lives.
TAKE: Below, a chamber that the water is filling and has not filled in three hundred years, and a mark on the floor set upside down on purpose.
LEADS: Upside down is the only one like it. The reason is written on the master mark at 0702.
:::

:::hex 0605 | The Assay House | Hills -- ring 3 -- four days
SEE: A stone house on a hillside with a chimney drawing, a scale in the window, and a path kept clear of grass.
HOOK: The eighty-first name on the muster lives here, has drawn a stipend for three hundred years, and weighs what the west sends down.
RISK: **Assayer Verity Small** is not hostile and not human any more, and will not be moved out of a post nobody has relieved her from.
TAKE: Everything. She has the whole line drawn, the tally of every mark, the reason for the Burn, and she will trade all of it for one thing: relief.
LEADS: Relief means a signed charter from the Office of Somnolent Maintenance. There is a stamp for that, in a sealed office, under Eldoria.
:::

:::hex 0703 | The Gate Tooth | Mountain -- ring 3 -- four days
SEE: The one pass through the eastern Teeth, a mark standing on either side of it, and old snow that nothing has walked on.
HOOK: Nothing gets through carrying no mark. Parties turn back at the pass without ever deciding to, and cannot say why afterwards.
RISK: DC 16 WIS to walk through unmarked, once per watch, or turn west and lose the day. Force it three times and something comes down to ask why.
TAKE: The northern mark is unset -- propped, not planted. Plant it and the pass is a road for everyone, forever.
LEADS: Through the pass is ring four: the waste at 0701, the Last Tooth at 0702, and the acre at 0704 that is getting bigger.
:::

## Ring four · five days, if you are quick · levels 9–11

0701 · 0702 · 0704 · 0705. Four hexes, and the whole campaign is in them. A party that arrives here casual, at ninth level, with a good plan and no marks, dies in the open. Do not soften ring four. It has been on the map, drawn and numbered, since the first night.

:::roll d8 | RING FOUR -- WHAT FINDS YOU
1 An **adult red dragon**'s hunting circuit, and it is not the worst thing here.
2 Ash weather. Two watches, no visibility, one exhaustion, and the ash is falling upward.
3 **Wraiths**, 2d4, walking a hex boundary that no longer has ground under it.
4 A **fire giant** with a mark on its back, going west, and it will not be turned.
5 The party's own camp from two nights ago, still burning, still occupied.
6 An **earth elemental**, ancient, that lifts a hand flat and waits to be shown a mark.
7 A hex that is not on the map, entered by accident, with weather and a horizon of its own.
8 Nothing at all, for two whole watches, in a place where that is the alarm.
:::

:::cols 2
:::hex 0701 | The North Margin | Waste -- ring 4 -- five days
SEE: Where the Burn stopped: living forest, then a line you could lay a rule against, then eight miles of ash three feet deep.
HOOK: Fire does not stop in a straight line. Something stopped it, along a line, and the line is the one the party has been mapping all campaign.
RISK: The ash is not ash and does not settle. Every watch, DC 15 CON or a level of exhaustion that a long rest will not lift, only a week in Kettlewick.
TAKE: Along the margin, every hundred yards, a mark -- set in a hurry, in 1189, some of them upside down, all of them holding.
LEADS: They were set walking east, and they get more hurried as they go. Follow them to 0704.
:::

:::hex 0702 | The Last Tooth | Mountain -- ring 4 -- five days
SEE: The highest peak on the map, and on its summit a single stone twice the height of a person, cut from the same chalk as every mark.
HOOK: The master mark. Every other stone in the west is set true to this one, and the whole line hangs off it.
RISK: Its keeper has been on this summit since 1189 and has not been relieved. It is beyond what a party can beat and knows precisely what it may do.
TAKE: The inscription: the survey's real charter, the date of the next re-setting, and the instruction to set the Mouth's mark inverted.
LEADS: The date on the stone is three years from now. The whole campaign has been a countdown nobody read until ring four.
:::

:::hex 0704 | The Unmade Acre | Waste -- ring 4 -- five days
SEE: An acre of nothing at the centre of the Burn. Not bare ground -- nothing, with an edge, and marks planted in a ring around it every ten feet.
HOOK: It is growing about a foot a year, and it has been growing since 1189, and the ring of marks is what has kept it to an acre.
RISK: Inside is not survivable and not describable. Outside is: the ring is missing four stones, and everything the campaign has been dragging home is a stone.
TAKE: Nothing. There is nothing in there to take, which is what the Grey is. The prize is that it stays an acre.
LEADS: Nowhere. This is the end of the map, and the campaign's last decision is made standing here with a blank stone and a mallet.
:::

:::hex 0705 | The Drawn Edge | Mountain -- ring 4 -- five days
SEE: The southeastern corner of the map, where the 1189 draughtsman ruled the sheet's edge -- and the land ends there too, in a clean vertical face of cloud.
HOOK: The map's edge is arbitrary. Somebody drew it. The land agreed. Nobody in Kettlewick will accept that report and Slake will pay for it anyway.
RISK: Walking east off the edge is not falling. It is being unfiled. A character who goes has never been chartered, and only the players remember them.
TAKE: The draughtsman's own kit, on a bench at the edge, with an unruled sheet and a pen that still has ink -- one hex, drawn once, is real.
LEADS: A drawn hex has a coordinate. Whatever the table writes in that box is where the next campaign starts.
:::

:::box dark | WHEN THE MAP IS FULL
Thirty-five hexes is about thirty-five sessions, which is a year of a weekly open table, and the end is not a boss. It is a decision made at 0704 by whoever happened to sign up that night: **set the four missing marks, or do not.**

Set them, and the west closes. The acre stops. The tariff is paid, the charter is discharged, Verity Small is relieved after three hundred years, and the Office of Somnolent Maintenance goes quiet again with four new names on its staff list. Nobody outside the table ever learns any of it happened.

Leave them, and the map is still true -- it just stops being a map of anywhere in about nine hundred years. Some tables choose this. Let them. Then hand out the blank sheet from 0705 and ask what the next campaign's 0103 is called.
:::
