:::pitch
Botany has leased a quarter-acre inside the Druid's Circle for sixty years and been locked out of it this month. Nobody will say why. Everybody knows why. The reason is in Row F and it is eating deer.
!! THEME -- everyone at this gate is lying politely, and one of the lies has teeth.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
Botany holds a lease on a quarter-acre inside the Druid's Circle and has held it sixty years. Access has been revoked. No reason has been given that survives contact with the lease.

We want people who can walk in, be pleasant, and walk out with the terms renewed. Do not threaten druids. Do not let Applied Thaumaturgy find out you went.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
Half a day east, in Silverweald: nine standing stones, a druid conclave older than the university, and a fenced quarter-acre Botany leases for growth trials. The lease runs another sixty years. The druids dispute this.

Deer have stopped crossing the plot and the smell carries in warm weather. A student went missing there in spring, though the registry shows she transferred to Highport and never wrote home. Applied Thaumaturgy denies paying anyone.
:::

:::box dark | HANDOUT -- THE LEASE, ONE CLAUSE UNDERLINED BY A PREVIOUS READER
**Cl. 11** -- Where a specimen exceeds the Tenant's own stated dimensions by more than one half, the Tenant shall attend in person to see it ended.

**This obligation does not pass to a hired party.**
:::
:::

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- SURVEY SKETCH
A planting plan on graph paper in a careful field hand. Nine stones as solid lozenges, north arrow, scale in yards, fence in ticks. Rows A to H, each with a specimen code and date. Three seasons of ink, from black to faded brown, pencil corrections, one thumbprint. Row F's label is struck through and rewritten four times, each larger and less controlled, the last running off the sheet. And the grid lines around Row F bow outward -- not shaky, drawn that way, deliberately, by someone recording a measurement. Margin: *Re-measured 3x. Fence unmoved. Plot now 27yd at the south end. Was 22.*
:::

:::secrets
1. Row F is a Botany cross that took. It has eaten deer for two seasons and it is still growing.
2. Clause 11 makes the Chair of Botany attend in person to see a specimen ended. The Chair is sixty-one and will not come.
3. The druids revoked access to force exactly that. They cannot end it inside their own stones without breaking their own law.
4. The missing student is in the cairn. The druids put her there, and they did it kindly, and they did it fast.
5. Applied Thaumaturgy is paying the conclave to hold out until Botany's funding lapses at the year's end.
6. The plot measures twenty-seven yards at the south end. The fence has not moved. The fence is not what grew.
7. Nobody here wants a fight. Eleven druids will stand in front of that gate until they starve, and they know you know.
8. The registry says she transferred to Highport. Somebody in the registry was asked to write that, and did.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT, THEN SAY NOTHING AND LET THEM TALK
Nine stones, a fenced quarter-acre inside them, and eleven druids standing between you and the gate. Nobody is armed. Nobody moves. The smell off the plot is sweet and wrong, like fruit going over in a hot room, and every single person here is pretending not to notice it.
:::

:::web THE WEB -- FIVE PLACES, NO ORDER
This is a conversation with five rooms in it. Three ways into every one, because they will get thrown out of the first two.
:::

:::cols 2
:::node A | The Gate | place | start
Where the conversation happens, and where it stays until somebody gives them a reason to move.
-> B  The smell comes off the plot in the warm and the gate faces straight into it.
-> C  One druid at the back will not look at the plot, and walks down to the camp at dusk.
-> D  Nine standing stones, and a tenth heap of stones that nobody stands near or names.
:::

:::node B | The Plot | place
Eight rows and Row F, which is not a row any more.
-> C  Row F has been fed deer for two seasons. The hides are cured and stacked in the camp.
-> D  The lease says the Tenant ends what it grows. Something has been ended here before, and buried.
-> E  Somebody in the treeline has counted these rows daily since spring, and left the grass flat doing it.
:::

:::node C | The Camp | place
Hides, smoke, and a child of about nine who will absolutely talk to you.
-> B  She will draw the plot from memory: eight rows and one that is fat in the middle.
-> D  She was told to stay off the cairn. She can tell you the exact week the telling started.
-> E  A purse in the elders' hut, university mint marks, Thaumaturgy's seal still on the string.
:::

:::node D | The Cairn | place
What the druids ended before, under stones stacked in a shape they use for one thing only.
-> B  Under the top course: a boot, and seed casings that match nothing in rows A to E.
-> C  They buried her without telling the camp. The camp found out anyway and one of them is sick with it.
-> E  Folded into the stones, in a university hand: *hold until the funding year ends.*
:::

:::node E | The Man in the Treeline | person
Thaumaturgy's, though he will call himself a surveyor. Paid weekly, patient, and here every day since spring.
:::
:::

:::valve
If it's dragging: a deer comes down to the fence line, and does not come back up it.
If it's going fast: the Chair's clerk arrives with a letter refusing to attend, and reads it aloud at the gate.
Cliffhanger: the smell is on the east road that night, half a mile from the stones.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- Row F is feeding and quiet about it. Nothing rustles until it is already too close.
- The druids will not strike first and will not move. Force gets you nothing and ends the lease.
- Thaumaturgy's man is in the treeline all day and takes notes on which of you talks.
- Anything handed to you in a cup. Refusing it is an insult. Drinking it is a save.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Produce the Chair. Sixty-one years old, terrified, and legally obliged.
- End Row F yourselves and argue Clause 11 afterwards, from a position of having done it.
- Show the druids who has been paying them, and let the conclave do the rest.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **The lease ends at the funding year.** No renewal, no argument, no Botany.
2. **The druids burn the plot**, as they have wanted to since spring.
3. **Row F is not in the plot when they do.** It has not been for a fortnight.
4. **It reaches the deer runs**, and then the east road, and the east road goes to Oakhaven.
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat Row F | CR 3 | AC 14 | HP 68 | SPD 5 ft | STR +4 | DEX -2 | CON +4 | INT -5 | WIS +1 | CHA -4
**Two tendrils** +6, 15 ft: 9 (2d4+4) bludgeoning and grappled (escape DC 14). It pulls 10 ft on its turn and does not let go.
**Root sense.** Blindsight 60 ft through soil. It knows where everyone standing on the plot is.
**False ground.** It fights from under two seasons of leaf litter and is unseen until it takes hold of somebody.
RESIST: piercing · **Vulnerable** fire, and the druids know it
**Sixty feet of it.** Cutting the crown does not kill it. Burning the south end does.
> The plot · one specimen · everything below Row F is the same plant
:::

:::npc Elder Sarn, Ninth Stone | Speaks for the conclave · seventy, unhurried, immovable
WANT: The thing in the plot ended by the people who grew it, inside the law, in front of witnesses.
VOICE: Slow, courteous, leaves long gaps you will want to fill. Do not fill them.
BREAK: She authorised the cairn in spring and has not slept properly since.
LINE: We are not refusing you the land. We are refusing to do your killing for you.
USE: She will tell the whole truth the moment somebody names Clause 11 out loud.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle Clause Eleven | the gate · a paperwork fight, run as a fight
LOOKS: A sixty-year lease, one clause underlined, and eleven druids who have clearly read it more recently than you have.
SOLVE: Get the Chair to the stones, or get the conclave to accept the party as the Tenant -- which needs a signature, a seal, and somebody willing to be Botany for an afternoon.
FAIL: Never a dead end. The conclave will hear one more argument at dusk, and dusk is when Row F feeds.
:::
