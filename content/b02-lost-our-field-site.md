:::pitch
Botany has leased a quarter-acre inside the Druid's Circle for sixty years and has been locked out of it since Tuesday. Nobody will say why. Everybody knows why. The why is in Row F, it is sixty feet long, and it has developed opinions about deer.
!! THEME -- everybody at this gate is being immaculately polite, and one of them is being polite about a shrub that eats livestock.
:::

:::read WHERE IT STARTS -- READ IT, THEN SAY NOTHING AND LET THEM TALK
Nine standing stones, a fenced quarter-acre inside them, and eleven druids shoulder to shoulder across the gate. Nobody is armed. Nobody moves. The smell off the plot is sweet and entirely wrong, like fruit going over in a hot room, and everybody here is pretending they cannot smell it.
:::

:::web THE WEB -- FIVE PLACES, NO ORDER
A conversation with five rooms in it, and they will be thrown out of the first two.
:::

:::cols 2
:::node A | The Gate | place | start
Where the conversation happens, and stays, until somebody gives them a reason to move.
-> B  The gate faces straight into the smell and nobody mentions it.
-> C  A druid at the back will not look at the plot, and goes down to the camp at dusk.
-> D  Nine standing stones, and a tenth little heap everybody walks around.

:::node B | The Plot | place
Eight tidy rows, and Row F, which stopped being a row last spring.
-> C  A deer a fortnight since March. Somebody is bringing the deer.
-> D  Something was ended here before and given a stone with a name on it.
-> E  A man in the treeline has counted these rows daily since spring.
:::

:::node C | The Camp | place
Hides, woodsmoke, and a nine-year-old who will tell you everything for a sweet.
-> B  She draws the plot from memory: eight rows and one fat in the middle.
-> D  She was told to keep off the cairn, and can name the week.
-> E  A purse in the elders' hut, university mint, Thaumaturgy's seal on the string.
:::

:::node D | The Cairn | place
Not a grave. A funeral, for Row E, two summers back, with full honours.
-> B  Seed casings under the top course, matching nothing in rows A to E.
-> C  The camp turned out in their good cloaks and half of them cried.
-> E  Folded in among the stones, in a university hand: *hold out until the funding year ends.*
:::

:::node E | The Man in the Treeline | person
Thaumaturgy's, though he says surveyor. Paid weekly, patient, here since spring.
-> C  He has been paying the conclave in coin he is not supposed to have.
:::
:::

+++

:::secrets
1. Row F is a Botany cross that took. Gloriously. Sixty feet of it, still going.
2. Clause 11 says the Tenant attends in person to see a specimen ended. The Chair is sixty-one and would rather die. The druids locked the gate to force exactly that -- they cannot end it inside their own stones without breaking their own law.
4. Applied Thaumaturgy is quietly paying the conclave to hold out until Botany's funding lapses in December.
5. The plot measures twenty-seven yards at the south end. The fence has not moved.
6. The student everyone whispers about transferred to Highport, is perfectly fine, and simply never writes. The druids find this the most sinister part of the whole business.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- Row F feeds quietly. Nothing rustles until it already has hold of somebody's ankle.
- The druids will not strike first and will not move. Force gets you nothing and ends the lease on the spot.
- Thaumaturgy's man is in the treeline all day, noting which of you does the talking.
- Three refusals and the conclave stops talking to you altogether.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Produce the Chair. Sixty-one, appalled, and legally obliged.
- End Row F yourselves and argue Clause 11 from the comfortable position of having done it.
- Show the conclave whose coin is in the elders' hut, then stand well back.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **The lease lapses in December**, and the druids burn the plot, which they have wanted to do since spring.
2. **Row F is not in it when they do.** It has not been for a fortnight.
3. **It reaches the deer runs, then the east road**, which goes to Oakhaven and the market gardens.
:::

:::cols 2
:::stat Row F | CR 3 | AC 14 | HP 68 | SPD 5 ft | STR +4 | DEX -2 | CON +4 | INT -5 | WIS +1 | CHA -4
**Two tendrils** +6, 15 ft: 9 (2d4+4) bludgeoning and grappled (DC 14). Pulls 10 ft a turn and does not let go.
**Root sense.** Blindsight 60 ft through soil, and it fights from under the leaf litter -- unseen until it already has somebody.
RESIST: piercing · **Vulnerable** fire, and the druids know it perfectly well
**Sixty feet of it.** Cutting the crown does nothing. Burning the south end does everything.
> The plot · one specimen · everything south of Row F is the same plant
:::

:::npc Elder Sarn, Ninth Stone | Speaks for the conclave · seventy, unhurried, immovable
WANT: The thing ended by the people who grew it, in the law, before witnesses.
VOICE: Slow, courteous, leaves gaps you will want to fill. Do not fill them.
BREAK: She gave Row E a funeral and meant every word of it.
USE: Tells the whole truth the moment somebody says *Clause Eleven* aloud.
LINE: We are not refusing you the land. We are refusing to do your killing for you.
:::
:::

:::cols 2
:::puzzle Clause Eleven | the gate · a paperwork fight, run as a fight
LOOKS: A sixty-year lease, one clause underlined, and eleven druids who have read it more recently than you have.
SOLVE: Get the Chair to the stones -- or get the conclave to accept the party *as* the Tenant, which wants a signature, a seal, and somebody willing to be Botany for an afternoon.
FAIL: Never a dead end. They hear one more argument at dusk, and dusk is when Row F feeds.
:::
