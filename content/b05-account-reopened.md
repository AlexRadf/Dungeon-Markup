:::pitch
The university has held the perpetual care contract on Kharn's crypt for two hundred years and performed none of it. Since January the crypt has started paying. Mortuary Studies would like to know what it is paying for, and who is going to be asked for the rest.
!! THEME -- a debt is a relationship, and somebody down there has been keeping their half of it.
:::

:::read ON THE BOARD -- AS POSTED
This department has held the perpetual care contract on the Crypt of Kharn for two hundred years and has received nothing.

Since January, payment has resumed. Silver, freshly struck, from no die we can identify, coming out through the offering slot in a sealed gate.

Find out who we are in business with. Divinity wants the site consecrated and closed and has no standing, having never paid the upkeep.
:::

:::cols 2
:::box plain | ON THE WAY THERE
A day and a half north-west, past Stonehaven, off the old road. Kharn was a tax collector, not a king, whatever Stonehaven tells you.

The crypt was sealed two hundred years ago and the university has held the maintenance contract since. There is a slot in the gate for offerings. Coin has been coming out of it since January. The lab says the silver is real and freshly struck. Stonehaven children dare each other to the gate.
:::

:::box dark | HANDOUT -- THE LAB'S ASSAY NOTE, SENT UP WITH THE JOB
Metal is silver, **.92 fine**. Die unrecorded in this department and appearing in no series struck in this kingdom. Wear consistent with a strike of under one year.

Three coins from this lot were **warm on receipt** and remain warm.
:::
:::

:::box plain | HANDOUT -- PEN & INK -- POSTER
A village bill, printed on a jobbing press in Stonehaven and nailed up for the fourth time -- two nail holes, torn upper corner, rain-blur through the lower third. Heavy woodcut border, badly justified, one line finished in a second face because the printer ran out of the first. *NOTICE -- TO PARENTS, FARMERS & DRIVERS -- KEEP OFF THE OLD GATE. Children found at the gate will be fined through their parents. Do not put your hand in the slot. Do not take what is in the slot. What comes out of the slot is not yours and the debt is not yours either.* Above it, a woodcut of the gate in a confident village hand; the slot is solid black, cut deeper than any other mark. Down the margin, a tally of dates in five hands over months. Two have small names beside them. The last is recent, and beside it, in a child's letters: *she came back out.*
:::

:::secrets
1. Kharn is paying for care the university has not performed in a hundred and thirty-six years. He does not know that. He is assuming good faith.
2. He is counting the arrears. When the sum is right, he comes up to collect the service, not the money.
3. The silver is struck below. Something down there is minting, from bullion nobody has brought in.
4. Taking the coin makes you the university's agent, and the work becomes yours. The ledger records the transfer within the hour.
5. Divinity's man came in spring, took a handful and walked out. He is still in Oakhaven and he is still on the books.
6. The hinges have been oiled. A gate sealed for two hundred years does not need hinges unless somebody intends to use them.
7. The gate opens outward. From the inside. It always did.
8. Nothing down there is decayed. Every surface is maintained to a standard the university has not met since the war.
:::

Sealed two hundred years, maintained the whole time. **Light:** none below the gate, and no dust in it either. Everything down here is swept, oiled and in good repair, which is the single most frightening fact on this page.

:::rooms | AREA | WHAT IS THERE
1 | The gate | Sealed, oiled, slot goes both ways. Warm silver under it, more every week. The hinges are wet. It opens **outward**.
2 | The counting room | Kharn's ledgers, still kept, in ink that has not dried. Bullion in and coin out on the same page. The arrears column runs to a figure with a date beside it, and the date is close.
3 | The mint | Press, dies, blanks, and finished silver too warm to bag. The dies are numbered to ledger lines. Nothing here is turning the press; it is turned from below, on a beat.
4 | The cell at the bottom | **Behind the secret door**, which is not locked and never was. Where the account is being settled. Kharn is in here, counting, and has been since January.
:::

:::roll d6 | COMPLICATION -- ONCE AN HOUR BELOW THE GATE

3 A Stonehaven child at the gate, alone.
4 A new entry appears in your handwriting.
5 Divinity's man arrives, wanting his half.
6 The hinges are oiled again behind you.

:::cols 2
:::box red | TROUBLE
- Nothing is decayed and everything is maintained. The place is not ruined, it is *staffed*.
- Take their coin and you are on the books. The ledger names you within the hour, correctly.
- Being on the books means the outstanding work is yours, and it is a hundred and thirty-six years of it.
- Whatever is doing the maintenance is doing it right now, in the next room, and does not stop.
:::

:::box gold | WAYS OUT
- Perform the maintenance, badly, and close the account. Two hundred years of arrears cleared in an afternoon.
- Take nothing at all, break the seal, and let Mortuary Studies argue with an empty crypt.
- Pay him in his own coin and let Finance explain to the Chancellor where it came from.
:::
:::

:::beats
1. **Payments keep rising.** He is compounding, and he is doing it correctly.
2. **The arrears settle in full**, and an account in credit is an account owed a service.
3. **The gate opens outward, from the inside**, which is what the hinges were oiled for.
4. **Kharn walks to Oakhaven** to be attended to, with the paperwork, in order, in his hand.
:::

:::stat Kharn, Warden of Arrears | CR 8 | AC 17 | HP 152 | SPD 30 ft | STR +4 | DEX +1 | CON +5 | INT +4 | WIS +5 | CHA +6
**Ledger touch** +8, 5 ft: 18 (4d6+4) necrotic, and the target's name is entered. Entered creatures cannot leave the crypt while the account stands.
**Called to account.** One creature within 60 ft, DC 16 CHA save or paralysed while he reads out what it has taken. Ends if it puts the item down.
SAVES: WIS +9, CHA +10 · **Immune** poison, exhaustion, frightened
**He will not strike first.** He states the sum, waits, and states it again. He fights only when the account is refused.
**Settle it and he stops.** Any honest discharge of the debt ends him, gratefully, mid-sentence.
> The cell · one · the fight is the failure state and he will tell you so
:::

**On shift.** It will not attack anyone who is working. Sweep, oil, polish, carry -- and it walks past you.
**Never stops.** Immune to being frightened or charmed. Reduced to 0 hp it puts its tools down neatly first.

:::puzzle The Perpetual Care Contract | the counting room · a day's work, or a fight
LOOKS: A hundred and thirty-six years of undone maintenance, itemised, in a hand that has never once been wrong.
SOLVE: Do some of it. Sweep, oil, re-letter a stone, log it, sign it. The contract does not specify a standard and Kharn is not a pedant. Signing it as the university closes the account.
FAIL: Signing it as yourselves transfers the contract. In perpetuity. You will be told this clearly and you will still do it.
:::

:::item One Coin of the New Die | Silver, .92 fine, warm -- the slot, or anywhere below
Warm as a hand, forever, and numbered to a line in a ledger you have now read. Spending it is fine. Keeping it means the account knows where you sleep, which is worth exactly as much as it costs. Mortuary Studies wants a third of these. Divinity wants them buried. Finance wants the handling fee.
:::
